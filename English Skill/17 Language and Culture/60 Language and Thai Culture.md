# Language and Thai Culture — ภาษากับวัฒนธรรมไทย

> *"The flip side of learning foreign culture: being able to present your own. A student who can explain Songkran in English becomes an ambassador."*

The curriculum pairs culture learning with culture sharing: students learn Anglophone culture (อ 2.1) and learn to **present Thai culture in English** (อ 2.2, courteous participation in intercultural exchange). This note covers the language of explaining Thai food, festivals, customs, and etiquette to foreigners — from simple ป.4 sentences ("This is a temple. Take off your shoes.") to ม.ปลาย tourism-level presentations and cultural comparison essays.

## 1 | Grade Band Breakdown

| Grade | Key Content |
|---|---|
| **ป.1–3** | Naming Thai things in English: food (pad thai, mango), objects (wai, temple), festivals (Songkran) |
| **ป.4–6** | Simple sentences about Thai customs: greetings, temple behavior, national symbols; "This is... / You should..." patterns |
| **ม.1–3** | Explaining and describing: how Songkran is celebrated, why Thais wai, Thai food descriptions; paragraphs and short presentations |
| **ม.4–6** | Comparative analysis (Thai vs Western values), cultural presentation projects, tourism English, discussing cultural preservation and change |

## 2 | Culture-Sharing Language Toolkit

| Function | ป.4–6 | ม.1–3 | ม.4–6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introducing | This is Songkran. | Songkran is celebrated in April. | Songkran, Thailand's traditional New Year, is celebrated from April 13–15. |
| Explaining why | We wai to show respect. | The wai shows respect based on age and status. | The wai reflects hierarchical values rooted in Buddhist culture. |
| Giving advice to visitors | You should take off your shoes. | Visitors are expected to dress politely at temples. | Visitors should avoid touching anyone's head, as it is considered sacred. |
| Comparing | Songkran is in April; Christmas is in December. | Unlike Western New Year, Songkran marks the solar new year. | While Western cultures celebrate individual achievement, Thai culture emphasizes community harmony. |

## 3 | Thai Cultural Topics and Their English Vocabulary

| Topic | Core vocabulary | Common learner problems |
|---|---|---|
| Food | pad thai, tom yum, som tam, spicy, fermented fish sauce | Untranslatable dishes: keep Thai name + explain ("som tam — a spicy green papaya salad") |
| Festivals | Songkran, Loy Krathong, Yi Peng, water blessing | Translating ทำบุญ, รดน้ำดำหัว — explain function, not word-for-word |
| Religion | temple (wat), monk, alms giving, ordination | Buddhist terms in English; respectful register |
| Etiquette | wai, respect for elders, hierarchy, saving face | "Saving face" concept in natural English |
| Performing arts | khon masked dance, muay thai, luk thung | Describing art forms with limited vocabulary |
| Crafts | silk weaving, celadon, wood carving | Regional vocabulary (Chiang Mai, Isan) |

## 5 | Common Errors When Presenting Thai Culture

| Error | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Word-for-word translation | "Songkran people play water" | Explain in natural English: "People celebrate Songkran by splashing water" |
| Unexplained Thai terms | "We do ทำบุญ in the morning" | Translate the function: "We make merit by offering food to monks" |
| Register mismatch | Casual slang in a tourism presentation | Match register to audience → [[31 Register & Formality]] |
| Missing structure | Facts without organization | Use presentation frameworks → [[38 Response Organization]] |
