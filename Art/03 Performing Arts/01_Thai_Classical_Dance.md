# Thai Classical Dance — นาฏศิลป์ไทย

Thai classical dance (นาฏศิลป์ไทย) is the refined movement tradition of the royal courts, built on a fixed vocabulary of postures and gestures that students learn step by step from primary school onward. This note helps parents understand what their child practises in class, from basic finger bends (จีบ) to full performance forms such as the standard circle dance (รำวงมาตรฐาน).

## 1 | Grade Band Breakdown

| Grade | Key Content |
|---|---|
| ป.1–3 | Basic movement (การเคลื่อนไหว), imitating animals and nature, dance vocabulary (นาฏยศัพท์) such as the finger bend (จีบ) and circle (วง), simple posture sequences, the wai (การไหว้) and greeting etiquette |
| ป.4–6 | Standard circle dance (รำวงมาตรฐาน), set dances (ระบำ), solo and paired dance (รำเดี่ยว, รำคู่), gesture language (ภาษาท่า) for storytelling, costume (เครื่องแต่งกาย) awareness |
| ม.1–3 | More complex repertoires, choreography principles, analysis of performance values, dance in historical and cultural context |
| ม.4–6 | Advanced solo and group composition, critique using Thai aesthetic standards, leadership in school productions, links between dance and national identity |

## 2 | Dance Vocabulary (นาฏยศัพท์)

Dance vocabulary (นาฏยศัพท์) is the shared technical language of Thai classical dance, and every student learns these basic terms in the early grades:

| Thai Term | English Meaning | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|---|
| จีบ | finger bend | fingertips pressed together, wrist bent |
| วง | circle | arm curved into a circle, palm facing outward |
| ตั้งวง | raised circle | arm circle held up beside the head |
| ส่งจีบ | extended finger bend | finger bend pushed forward and away |
| ฉีก | spread | fingers spread apart, palm open |
| กระทุ้ง | jab | sharp forward push of the hand |

The body must stay soft and flowing (ความอ่อนโยน), with knees bent and heels grounded, because Thai classical dance values smoothness and control over speed or force.

## 3 | Gesture Language (ภาษาท่า)

Gesture language (ภาษาท่า) lets dancers speak without words. Each gesture is a conventional sign that audiences learn to read:

- Walking (เดิน), flying (บิน) and swimming are shown through foot patterns and arm shapes.
- Emotions such as love (รัก), anger (โกรธ) and sadness (เศร้า) each have their own gestures and facial expressions (สีหน้า).
- The wai (การไหว้) appears in dance as a sign of respect, just as it does in daily Thai life.

Because the meaning is conventional, children practise until the gesture becomes automatic, and only then add feeling on top of it.

## 4 | Performance Forms and Standard Repertoire

Thai classical performance is divided into broad forms that appear across the curriculum:

| Form | Thai | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Standard circle dance | รำวงมาตรฐาน | A social dance with 10 fixed dances (เพลงรำวง), each with set steps; the first full form most students learn |
| Set dance | ระบำ | Group dances with fixed choreography, such as the set dance of the epic (ระบำกฤดาภินิหาร) |
| Solo dance | รำเดี่ยว | One dancer performing a complete story or mood |
| Paired dance | รำคู่ | Two dancers moving in mirror-like harmony |

Costumes (เครื่องแต่งกาย) follow the role: crowns and pointed headdresses for heroes, layered silk skirts (ผ้านุ่ง) for heroines. At school level, simplified versions are used so children can move freely.

## 5 | Etiquette and Values (มารยาทและคุณค่า)

Learning Thai classical dance also means learning manners (มารยาท):

- Students wai (ไหว้) their teacher before and after class and show respect for the art (ความเคารพ).
- Practice builds patience (ความอดทน), gentleness (ความอ่อนโยน) and concentration (สมาธิ).
- Performing for parents and school audiences builds confidence and pride in Thai culture (ความภาคภูมิใจ).
