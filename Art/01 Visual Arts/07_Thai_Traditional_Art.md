# Thai Traditional Art — ศิลปะไทย

Thai traditional art (ศิลปะไทย) is the art of the kingdom: flowing patterns (ลายไทย), temple murals (จิตรกรรมฝาผนัง), gilded sculpture, and the fine crafts (งานประณีตศิลป์) passed down for centuries. The curriculum asks every Thai child to know, value, and help preserve (การอนุรักษ์) this heritage.

## 2 | Thai Patterns (ลายไทย)

| Pattern | Thai | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Kranok | ลายกระหนก | The classic flame-like curve used everywhere in Thai art |
| Kanok | ลายกนก | Flowing foliage scrolls, the same family as kranok |
| Prajam Yarm | ลายประจำยาม | A four-way symmetrical flower motif |
| Thep and mythical animals | เทพและสัตว์หิมพานต์ | Celestial beings and creatures of the Himavanta forest |

Thai patterns appear on temple doors, on the borders of official documents, on wedding trays (พาน), and even on the costumes of Thai classical dancers. Children first trace them, then draw them freehand, and finally use them in their own compositions.

## 3 | Thai Painting and Murals (จิตรกรรมไทยและจิตรกรรมฝาผนัง)

Thai painting is flat and decorative rather than realistic: figures in profile, no shadows, and backgrounds filled with patterns and gold. Temple murals (จิตรกรรมฝาผนัง) tell the stories of the Buddha's lives (ชาดก), the Ramakien (รามเกียรติ์), and scenes of daily life in the past. Traditional techniques include mineral colors (สีจากแร่), black lacquer backgrounds (พื้นสีดำ), and gold leaf (การปิดทอง) applied with great patience.

## 4 | Thai Crafts, Architecture, and Preservation

| Craft | Thai | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nielloware | เครื่องถม | Silver inlaid with black alloy, from the South |
| Lacquerware | เครื่องเขิน | Black and gold lacquered boxes |
| Thai silk | ผ้าไหม | Hand-woven cloth, especially from the Northeast |
| Wood carving | งานแกะสลักไม้ | Temple doors and furniture |

Thai architecture (สถาปัตยกรรมไทย) means the tiered roofs (หลังคาซ้อนชั้น) of temples and palaces and the gable finials (ช่อฟ้า) that curve upward like birds. Preservation (การอนุรักษ์) includes caring for temple art, reviving dying crafts, and visiting museums such as the National Museum (พิพิธภัณฑสถานแห่งชาติ). Modern Thai artists continue these traditions in new forms, and the curriculum links the past to today.
