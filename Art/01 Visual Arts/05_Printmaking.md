# Printmaking — ภาพพิมพ์

Printmaking (ภาพพิมพ์) is making art by stamping, pressing, or transferring ink so that many copies of one image can be made. From potato prints in Grade 1 to silk-screen printing (ภาพพิมพ์ซิลค์สกรีน) in high school, children learn that art can be repeated, shared, and mass-produced.

## 1 | Grade Band Breakdown

| Grade | Key Content |
|---|---|
| ป.1–3 | Stamping (การประทับตรา), potato and vegetable prints, leaf prints (ภาพพิมพ์ใบไม้) |
| ป.4–6 | Block printing (ภาพพิมพ์บล็อก), simple relief prints from cardboard and foam |
| ม.1–3 | Rubber and linoleum block prints, monoprint (ภาพพิมพ์เดี่ยว), careful registration |
| ม.4–6 | Screen printing (ภาพพิมพ์ซิลค์สกรีน), multi-color prints, printmaking as heritage and career |

## 2 | Simple Printmaking Techniques

| Technique | Thai | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Stamping | การประทับตรา | Pressing an inked object onto paper |
| Vegetable prints | ภาพพิมพ์ผัก | Halved potatoes, okra, and lotus roots make natural patterns |
| Leaf prints | ภาพพิมพ์ใบไม้ | Painting a leaf and pressing it onto paper |
| Rubbings | การถูสำริด | Rubbing crayon over paper on a textured surface |
| String prints | ภาพพิมพ์เชือก | Gluing string to cardboard and printing the raised lines |
| Monoprint | ภาพพิมพ์เดี่ยว | One unique print pulled from a painted plate |

Each printed copy is called a proof (ภาพพิมพ์หนึ่งแผ่น), and a set of identical proofs from one block is an edition (การพิมพ์เป็นชุด). Children love the surprise of pulling a print: the image appears in reverse, which teaches careful planning and patience.

## 3 | Block and Screen Printing

Block printing (ภาพพิมพ์บล็อก) cuts a design into a flat surface: rubber, linoleum (ยางแกะ), or foam board. Ink is rolled on with a brayer (ลูกกลิ้ง) and paper is pressed down by hand or with a spoon. The raised parts print; the cut-away parts stay white.

Screen printing (ภาพพิมพ์ซิลค์สกรีน) pushes ink through a fine mesh with a squeegee, the same process used for T-shirts (เสื้อยืด), posters, and fabric. High school students learn to print in two or more colors by registering each layer carefully so the colors line up.

## 4 | Printmaking as Thai Heritage

Thailand has a long printmaking tradition, from the woodblock prints that illustrated old Thai folding books (สมุดไทย) to contemporary Thai print artists who exhibit internationally. Traditional cloth stamping with carved wooden blocks (การพิมพ์ผ้าด้วยแม่พิมพ์ไม้) is still practiced in local craft communities. Schools often connect printmaking projects to festivals such as Loy Krathong (ลอยกระทง), printing krathong and lantern designs for the celebration.
