# 03 — Developmental & Learning Psychology

> *"You can't teach a child you don't understand. Developmental psychology tells you who they are. Learning psychology tells you how they learn."*

This domain bridges psychology and education — understanding how children grow, think, and learn so that teaching meets them where they are. Taught in **Years 1–2**.

---

## 1 | Child & Adolescent Development

### 1.1 Piaget's Cognitive Development

| Stage | Age | Key Features | Teaching Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sensorimotor** | 0–2 yr | Learning through senses and actions; object permanence | Sensory play, peek-a-boo |
| **Preoperational** | 2–7 yr | Symbolic thinking, egocentrism, animism, no conservation | Concrete materials, hands-on, imaginative play |
| **Concrete Operational** | 7–11 yr | Logical thinking about concrete objects; conservation, classification, reversibility | Manipulatives, experiments, organized activities |
| **Formal Operational** | 11+ yr | Abstract, hypothetical, systematic reasoning | Debates, hypothetical problems, scientific method |

### 1.2 Vygotsky's Sociocultural Theory

| Concept | Definition | Teaching Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)** | Distance between what learner can do alone vs. with help | Scaffold instruction — provide just enough support |
| **Scaffolding** | Temporary support that is gradually removed | Modeling → guided practice → independent practice |
| **More Knowledgeable Other (MKO)** | Anyone with more understanding — teacher, peer, parent, even technology | Peer tutoring, collaborative learning |
| **Language & Thought** | Language is the primary tool of intellectual adaptation | Discussion, think-aloud, verbal reasoning |

> *"What a child can do in cooperation today, he can do alone tomorrow."* — Lev Vygotsky

### 1.3 Erikson's Psychosocial Stages (Relevant to School Years)

| Stage | Age | Crisis | School Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Initiative vs. Guilt** | 3–6 yr | Taking initiative; guilt when overstepping | Encourage curiosity; safe risk-taking; choice |
| **Industry vs. Inferiority** | 6–12 yr | Competence/mastery vs. feeling inadequate | Praise effort, not just results; achievable challenges |
| **Identity vs. Role Confusion** | 12–18 yr | "Who am I?" — identity formation | Career exploration, role models, identity-safe space |

### 1.4 Physical Development & Classroom Implications

| Age | Motor Development | Classroom Consideration |
|---|---|---|
| **Early childhood (3–6)** | Gross motor developing rapidly; fine motor still clumsy | Large pencils, big spaces, movement breaks |
| **Middle childhood (6–12)** | Fine motor refines; growth steady | Handwriting practice, sports, active play |
| **Early adolescence (12–15)** | Puberty; rapid growth; awkward coordination | Body image sensitivity; need for movement; fatigue |
| **Late adolescence (15–18)** | Adult-like physical maturity | Long periods of sitting manageable |

## 2 | Learning Theories

### 2.1 Behaviorism

| Concept | Classroom Application |
|---|---|
| **Classical Conditioning (Pavlov)** | Associate learning with positive experiences (classroom as safe, happy place) |
| **Operant Conditioning (Skinner)** | Reinforcement (praise, tokens, grades), punishment (use sparingly and thoughtfully) |
| **Schedules of Reinforcement** | Variable ratio (most motivating) — surprise rewards, not every time |
| **Shaping** | Reinforce successive approximations — celebrate small steps |
| **Premack Principle** | "First do the hard thing, then the fun thing" (homework before free time) |

### 2.2 Cognitivism

| Concept | Theorist | Classroom Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Information Processing Model** | Atkinson & Shiffrin | Sensory memory → Working memory → Long-term memory. Chunk content; avoid overload |
| **Schema Theory** | Piaget, Bartlett | Connect new info to existing knowledge; activate prior knowledge |
| **Meaningful Learning** | Ausubel | Use advance organizers; relate to students' lives |
| **Discovery Learning** | Bruner | Let students figure things out — guided discovery |

### 2.3 Constructivism

| Type | Key Idea | Classroom Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive Constructivism** (Piaget) | Learners construct knowledge individually through experience | Hands-on exploration, experiments |
| **Social Constructivism** (Vygotsky) | Knowledge constructed through social interaction | Group work, discussion, peer teaching |
| **Constructionism** (Papert) | Learning by making — creating tangible artifacts | Makerspaces, projects, coding, building |

## 3 | Motivation (การสร้างแรงจูงใจ)

### 3.1 Types of Motivation

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intrinsic** | Doing something because it's inherently interesting/enjoyable | Reading for pleasure; solving puzzles for fun |
| **Extrinsic** | Doing something for external reward or to avoid punishment | Studying for grades; working for money |
| **Amotivation** | Lack of intention to act | "I don't care about this subject" |

### 3.2 Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)

| Need | Description | How to Foster |
|---|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | Need to feel in control of own behavior | Offer choices; explain why; minimize controlling language |
| **Competence** | Need to feel capable and effective | Optimal challenge (not too easy, not too hard); constructive feedback |
| **Relatedness** | Need to feel connected to others | Warm teacher-student relationships; collaborative learning |

### 3.3 Growth Mindset vs. Fixed Mindset (Carol Dweck)

| Fixed Mindset | Growth Mindset |
|---|---|
| "I'm just not good at math" | "I can't do this math... YET" |
| Intelligence is fixed | Intelligence can grow with effort |
| Avoids challenges to protect ego | Embraces challenges to learn |
| Gives up easily | Persists through setbacks |
| Effort = "I must be dumb" | Effort = path to mastery |
| Threatened by others' success | Inspired by others' success |

**How teachers foster growth mindset:** Praise effort, strategies, and persistence — NOT "you're so smart."

### 3.4 Attribution Theory (Weiner)

| Attribution | Effect on Motivation |
|---|---|
| **Internal + Controllable** (effort) | "I failed because I didn't study enough" → Next time, study more 💪 |
| **Internal + Uncontrollable** (ability) | "I failed because I'm stupid" → Helplessness 😞 |
| **External + Controllable** | "I failed because the teacher was unfair" → Blame 😤 |
| **External + Uncontrollable** (luck) | "I failed because I was unlucky" → No action 🤷 |

**Teacher goal:** Redirect students from "I'm not smart enough" to "I need a better strategy."

## 4 | Individual Differences

### 4.1 Intelligence

| Theory | Key Idea |
|---|---|
| **Spearman's g** | General intelligence underlies all cognitive abilities |
| **Gardner's Multiple Intelligences** | 8 types: linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalistic |
| **Sternberg's Triarchic** | Analytical, Creative, Practical intelligence |

### 4.2 Learning Styles — The Caution

> ⚠️ **The "learning styles" myth:** Despite popularity (visual/auditory/kinesthetic), research shows NO evidence that matching teaching to preferred "style" improves learning. What works: **multiple modalities for ALL students**, not matching to individual styles.

### 4.3 Learning Differences That DO Matter

| Factor | Teaching Response |
|---|---|
| **Prior knowledge** | Pre-assess; fill gaps before new content |
| **Working memory capacity** | Chunk information; provide written supports |
| **Processing speed** | Allow extra time; reduce quantity, not quality |
| **Language background** | Scaffold vocabulary; use visuals; peer support for ELL |
| **Interest** | Connect content to student interests; offer choice |
| **Self-regulation** | Teach study skills, goal-setting, time management |
