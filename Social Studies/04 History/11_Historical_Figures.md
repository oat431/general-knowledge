# Historical Figures — บุคคลสำคัญทางประวัติศาสตร์

> *"The history of a nation is the sum of the stories of its people."*

This note covers important non-royal figures in Thai and world history. Students learn about national heroes, reformers, cultural leaders, and key figures in democratic movements. Beyond monarchs, these individuals shaped Thailand through military leadership, political reform, arts, and social change.

## 2 | Thai National Heroes (วีรบุรุษและวีรสตรี)

### Thao Suranari / Ya Mo (ท้าวสุรนารี / ย่าโม)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Real name** | Mo (โม) |
| **Title** | Thao Suranari (conferred by Rama III) |
| **Lived** | พ.ศ. 2314–2395 (1771–1852) |
| **Role** | Wife of deputy governor of Nakhon Ratchasima (Korat) |
| **Heroic act** | Saved Korat from Lao invasion (1826/พ.ศ. 2369) |
| **Legacy** | Statue in Korat, annual festival |

**The Story:** During Anouvong's rebellion (Lao king of Vientiane attacked Korat), Ya Mo is credited with rallying women to fight and tricking Lao soldiers with food and drink, enabling Thai forces to prevail.

### Sunthorn Phu (สุนทรภู่)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Lived** | พ.ศ. 2329–2398 (1786–1855) |
| **Title** | "Shakespeare of Thailand" |
| **Role** | Royal poet, greatest Thai literary figure |
| **Era** | Rattanakosin (Rama II–IV) |
| **Famous works** | Nirat Phra That, Phra Aphai Mani (พระอภัยมณี) |
| **UNESCO** | Named Great Personality of the World (1986) |

### Thao Thep Kasattri and Thao Si Sunthon (ท้าวเทพกระษัตรีและท้าวศรีสุนทร)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Sisters who defended Phuket against Burmese invasion |
| **Year** | พ.ศ. 2328 (1785) |
| **Heroic act** | Rallied women to disguise as soldiers, deterred Burmese attack |
| **Legacy** | Monument in Phuket, heroines of the South |

### King Pinklao (กรมพระราชวังบวรวิไชยชาญ)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Front Palace (Viceroy) under Rama IV |
| **Known for** | Modern military, science, foreign languages |
| **Significance** | Modernizer alongside Rama IV |

### Chao Phraya Bodindecha (เจ้าพระยาบดินทรเดชา)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Real name** | Singh (สิงห์) |
| **Role** | Military commander under Rama III |
| **Campaigns** | Suppressed Ho rebellion, fought in Cambodia, Vientiane |
| **Legacy** | One of greatest military leaders of early Rattanakosin |

## 3 | Democratic and Political Figures

### Pridi Banomyong (ปรีดี พนมยงค์-correct transliteration: ปรีดี พนมยงค์)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Born** | May 11, พ.ศ. 2443 (May 11, 1900) |
| **Role** | Leader of Khana Ratsadon (People's Party), 1932 Revolution |
| **Positions** | Prime Minister, Regent, Senior Statesman |
| **Contributions** | Drafted first constitution, economic plan, education reform |
| **Title** | "Statesman of the Century" (รัฐบุรุษแห่งศตวรรษ) |

### Field Marshal Phibun Songkhram (จอมพล ป. พิบูลสงคราม)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Military leader, Prime Minister (1938–1944, 1948–1957) |
| **Nationalism** | Promoted Thai nationalism, changed country name to Thailand |
| **Cultural mandates** | 12 mandates on dress, language, customs |
| **WWII** | Allied with Japan, later switched to Allies |
| **Legacy** | Controversial: modernizer vs authoritarian |

### Field Marshal Sarit Thanarat (จอมพล สฤษดิ์ ธนะรัชต์)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Military dictator (1957–1963) |
| **Policies** | Economic development, promoted monarchy |
| **Legacy** | Revived King's role in public life, authoritarian rule |

## 7 | Upper Secondary (ม.4-6): Biographical Analysis

### Evaluating Historical Figures

| Question | Consideration |
|---|---|
| **Context** | What was the historical situation? |
| **Options** | What choices did they have? |
| **Impact** | What were short-term vs long-term effects? |
| **Sources** | Who wrote about them, and why? |
| **Legacy** | How are they remembered today? |
| **Controversy** | Is the hero narrative contested? |

### Hero-Making in History

| Process | Description |
|---|---|
| **Mythologization** | Real deeds become legendary over time |
| **Political use** | Figures used to support current political narratives |
| **National identity** | Heroes serve as symbols of national character |
| **Revisionism** | Later scholars challenge heroic narratives |
| **Critical balance** | Acknowledge achievements + flaws |

## 9 | Real-World Examples

- **Ya Mo Festival** — Annual celebration in Nakhon Ratchasima (March–April)
- **Sunthorn Phu Day** — June 26, celebrated as Sunthorn Phu Day since 1986
- **Statues and monuments** — Throughout Thailand commemorating heroes
- **Museum exhibits** — Many provinces have museums dedicated to local heroes
- **Cultural performances** — Likay, theatre performances about hero stories
