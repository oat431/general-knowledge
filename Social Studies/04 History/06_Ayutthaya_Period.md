# Ayutthaya Period — สมัยอยุธยา

> *"The city so great and wealthy that no monarch in the East could be compared with him."* — Jeremias Van Vliet, Dutch trader

The Ayutthaya Period (1351–1767 CE / พ.ศ. 1894–2310) was Thailand's longest-lasting kingdom — 417 years, 33 kings, and 5 dynasties. Students learn about the sophisticated bureaucratic system (sakdina), international trade that made Ayutthaya one of the world's largest cities, and the great warrior-kings Naresuan and Narai.

## 3 | Governance: Sakdina System (ระบบศักดินา)

| Concept | Thai | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Sakdina** | ศักดินา | Numerical rank system determining status and land rights |
| **Chatusadom** | จตุสดมภ์ | Four central ministries |
| **Prachatambon** | ประชาบดี | Provincial administration |

### Chatusadom (Four Ministries)

| Ministry | Thai | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Wieng** | เวียง | Interior, royal household |
| **Wang** | วัง | Palace affairs, capital city |
| **Klang** | คลัง | Finance, treasury |
| **Na** | นา | Agriculture, lands |

### Sakdina Ranks

| Rank | Sakdina Value | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **King** | 100,000 | Supreme ruler |
| **Uparat (Viceroy)** | 20,000-50,000 | Heir apparent |
| **Samuha Nayok** | 10,000 | Prime minister (civil) |
| **Samuha Kalahom** | 10,000 | Prime minister (military) |
| **Khun Luang** | 400-1,000 | Nobles |
| **Phrai** | 10-25 | Commoners (corvée labor) |
| **That** | 5 | Slaves |

## 4 | King Naresuan the Great (สมเด็จพระนเรศวรมหาราช)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Reign** | พ.ศ. 2133–2148 (1590–1605 CE) |
| **Father** | King Maha Thammaracha |
| **Childhood** | Hostage in Burma (Hongsawadi) from age 9 |
| **Known for** | Declared independence from Burma, warrior king |

### Key Events

| Event | Thai | Year | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Declaration of independence** | การประกาศอิสรภาพ | พ.ศ. 2127 (1584) | Ended Burmese vassalage |
| **Elephant Battle (Don Chedi)** | ยุทธหัตถี | พ.ศ. 2135 (1592) | Defeated Crown Prince of Burma in single combat |
| **Conquered Hongsawadi** | ตีหงสาวดี | พ.ศ. 2138–2139 | Sacked Burmese capital |
| **Royal Karen Chronicle** | ตำนานฉบับกะเหรี่ยงหลวง | — | Source for Naresuan's campaigns |

### Naresuan's Military Legacy

| Achievement | Description |
|---|---|
| **Reunified kingdom** | Recovered territories lost to Burma |
| **Naval expansion** | Built fleet to project power |
| **Foreign mercenaries** | Employed Japanese, Portuguese, Chinese soldiers |
| **Annual campaigns** | 9 major military campaigns against Burma |

## 5 | King Narai the Great (สมเด็จพระนารายณ์มหาราช)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Reign** | พ.ศ. 2199–2231 (1656–1688 CE) |
| **Known for** | Golden age of diplomacy, relations with France |
| **Greek advisor** | Constantine Phaulkon (เจ้าพระยาวิชาเยนทร์) |

### Foreign Relations

| Country | Nature of Relations |
|---|---|
| **France** | Exchanged ambassadors with Louis XIV (1685, 1687) |
| **Netherlands (Dutch)** | Trade rivalry, armed conflict (1664) |
| **England** | East India Company factory (1612) |
| **Portugal** | Oldest Western relations |
| **China** | Tribute trade |
| **Japan** | Japanese quarter, mercenary community |
| **Persia** | Shia Muslim community |

### Greek Advisor: Constantine Phaulkon

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | Greek, born on Cephalonia |
| **Arrival** | Came to Ayutthaya via English East India Company |
| **Role** | Prime minister, foreign affairs |
| **Religion** | Catholic, promoted missionary activity |
| **Fall** | Executed during 1688 revolution (Siamese revolution) |

## 8 | Fall of Ayutthaya (การเสียกรุงศรีอยุธยา)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Date** | April 7, พ.ศ. 2310 (April 7, 1767) |
| **Conqueror** | King Hsinbyushin of Burma |
| **Duration of siege** | 14 months |
| **Aftermath** | City burned, population deported, libraries destroyed |

### Causes of Fall

| Factor | Description |
|---|---|
| **Internal conflict** | Succession disputes, weak kings |
| **Economic decline** | Loss of trade revenue |
| **Military weakness** | Outdated defense system |
| **Burmese strength** | Powerful, well-organized army |

## 9 | Upper Secondary (ม.4-6): Historical Debates

### Was Ayutthaya "Declining"?

| View | Argument |
|---|---|
| **Decline thesis** | Weak kings, factionalism led to fall |
| **Revisionist view** | Ayutthaya was prosperous until the final Burmese invasion |
| **Balanced view** | Internal weakness + external Burmese strength |

### The "Two Falls" of Ayutthaya

| Event | Year | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **First fall** | พ.ศ. 2296 (1753) | Briefly captured, recovered |
| **Second (final) fall** | พ.ศ. 2310 (1767) | Permanently destroyed |

### Comparative: Ayutthaya's Governance

| Feature | Ayutthaya | Modern Thailand |
|---|---|---|
| **Bureaucracy** | Chatusadom (4 ministries) | 20+ ministries |
| **Social hierarchy** | Sakdina (numerical ranks) | Constitutional equality |
| **Succession** | Royal blood + court politics | Constitutional law |
| **Taxation** | Corvée labor (days/year) | Money taxes |
