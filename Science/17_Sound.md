# Sound — เสียง

> *"Music is the arithmetic of sounds as optics is the geometry of light."* — Claude Debussy

This note covers the production, properties, and behavior of sound waves. Students progress from identifying sounds to understanding frequency, pitch, and sound measurement.

## 3 | Sound Production and Transmission

### 3.1 How Sound is Produced

```mermaid
graph LR
    A[วัตถุสั่น / Object vibrates] --> B[โมเลกุลอากาศสั่น / Air molecules vibrate]
    B --> C[คลื่นเสียง / Sound wave]
    C --> D[หูรับเสียง / Ear receives sound]
    D --> E[สมองแปลผล / Brain interprets]
```

**Key principle:** Sound is produced by vibrating objects and needs a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to travel.

### 3.2 Sound Cannot Travel in a Vacuum

Demonstration: Bell jar experiment — as air is removed, sound becomes fainter.


### 4.2 Wave Equation

$$v = f\lambda$$

Where:
- $v$ = wave speed (m/s)
- $f$ = frequency (Hz)
- $\lambda$ = wavelength (m)

## 5 | Speed of Sound

### 5.1 Speed in Different Media

| Medium | Speed (m/s) | Thai |
|---|---|---|
| Air (20°C) | 343 | อากาศ |
| Water | 1,480 | น้ำ |
| Steel | 5,960 | เหล็ก |
| Wood | 3,300-4,000 | ไม้ |

**Rule:** Sound travels fastest in solids, slower in liquids, slowest in gases.

### 5.2 Factors Affecting Speed in Air

$$v = 331 + 0.6T$$

Where $T$ = temperature in °C

## 7 | Sound Behavior

### 7.1 Reflection (การสะท้อน)

- **Echo (เสียงสะท้อน):** Sound bouncing off surfaces
- Minimum distance for distinct echo: ~17 m (sound travels to surface and back in 0.1 s)
- Used in sonar and ultrasound imaging

### 7.2 Resonance (การสั่นพ้อง)

- Objects vibrate at their **natural frequency** when energy is applied
- Matching frequency causes **amplification**
- Example: Opera singer breaking glass
