# Oceanography — สมุทรศาสตร์

> *"We know more about the surface of the Moon than the deep ocean floor."* — Robert Ballard, discoverer of the *Titanic*

Oceanography (สมุทรศาสตร์) is the scientific study of the oceans, covering their physical, chemical, biological, and geological aspects. Earth's oceans cover about $71\%$ of the planet's surface and contain $97\%$ of all water. The oceans regulate global climate, provide food for billions, and harbor immense biodiversity (ความหลากหลายทางชีวภาพ).

The ocean is not uniform — it has distinct zones based on depth, light penetration, and distance from shore. Ocean currents (กระแสน้ำทะเล) act as a giant conveyor belt redistributing heat from equator to poles. Understanding tides (ปรากฏการณ์น้ำขึ้นน้ำลง), waves (คลื่น), and ocean chemistry is crucial for coastal management, fisheries, and understanding climate systems.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Ocean Zones (เขตต่างๆ ของมหาสมุทร)

Oceans are classified by two systems — **horizontal** (by distance from shore) and **vertical** (by depth/light).

**Horizontal zones:**

| Zone | Definition | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Neritic** (เนอริติก) | Over continental shelf ($<200$ m) | Sunlit, nutrient-rich, high productivity |
| **Oceanic** (สมุทรศาสตร์น้ำลึก) | Beyond continental shelf | Deep, vast, low nutrient |

**Vertical zones:**

| Zone | Depth | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Epipelagic** (ซันไลต์) | $0$–$200$ m | Sunlit zone; photosynthesis |
| **Mesopelagic** (ทไวไลต์) | $200$–$1000$ m | Dim light; twilight zone |
| **Bathypelagic** (มืด) | $1000$–$4000$ m | No sunlight; high pressure |
| **Abyssopelagic** | $4000$–$6000$ m | Near-freezing, near-dark |
| **Hadalpelagic** | $>6000$ m | Deep ocean trenches |

The **benthic zone** (เขตเบนธิก) is the ocean floor at all depths; the **pelagic zone** (เขตเปลวิก) is the open water column.

### 3.2 Ocean Currents (กระแสน้ำทะเล)

**Surface currents (กระแสน้ำผิว)** are driven by:
1. **Global winds** (ลมประจำ) — trade winds, westerlies
2. **Coriolis effect** (ปรากฏการณ์คอริออลิส) — deflects currents
3. **Continental deflection** — landmasses redirect flow

Surface currents form large circular **gyres** (วงหมุน):

| Gyre | Hemisphere | Rotation |
|---|---|---|
| North Pacific | Northern | Clockwise |
| South Pacific | Southern | Counter-clockwise |
| North Atlantic | Northern | Clockwise |
| South Atlantic | Southern | Counter-clockwise |

**Thermohaline circulation (การไหลเวียนเทอร์โมฮาลีน)** is the global "conveyor belt" driven by water density differences:
- Cold, salty water at poles **sinks** (high density)
- Warm, less salty water at equator **rises**
- This deep circulation takes ~$1{,}000$ years to complete one cycle

### 3.3 Tides (ปรากฏการณ์น้ำขึ้นน้ำลง)

Tides are the periodic rise and fall of sea level caused by the **gravitational pull** (แรงโน้มถ่วง) of the Moon and Sun.

**Spring tides (น้ำขึ้นสูงสุด):**
- Occur at **new moon** and **full moon**
- Sun, Moon, and Earth are aligned
- Gravitational forces add together → **highest high tide, lowest low tide**

**Neap tides (น้ำขึ้นน้อยสุด):**
- Occur at **first quarter** and **last quarter** moon
- Sun and Moon pull at $90^\circ$ to each other
- Forces partially cancel → **lowest tidal range**

The Moon's gravitational effect is about **twice** that of the Sun, despite the Sun's greater mass, because the Moon is much closer.

### 3.4 Ocean Waves (คลื่นในมหาสมุทร)

Waves are energy traveling through water — water particles move in **circular orbits** but do not travel with the wave.

$$v = \sqrt{g \cdot d} \quad \text{(shallow water wave)}$$

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{g\lambda}{2\pi}} \quad \text{(deep water wave)}$$

where $v$ = wave speed, $g$ = gravity, $d$ = depth, $\lambda$ = wavelength.

When waves approach shallow water, they **slow down**, wavelength decreases, and wave height increases until the wave **breaks** (พังทลาย).

### 3.5 Ocean Chemistry (เคมีมหาสมุทร)

**Salinity (ความเค็ม)** averages $35\ ‰$ (parts per thousand). Major ions:
- Chloride ($\ce{Cl^-}$): $55\%$
- Sodium ($\ce{Na^+}$): $31\%$
- Sulfate ($\ce{SO4^{2-}}$), Magnesium ($\ce{Mg^{2+}}$), Calcium ($\ce{Ca^{2+}}$), Potassium ($\ce{K^+}$)

**Ocean pH** averages about $8.1$ — slightly alkaline. Ocean acidification (การเป็นกรดของมหาสมุทร) occurs as the ocean absorbs excess $\ce{CO2}$:

$$\ce{CO2 + H2O -> H2CO3 -> H+ + HCO3^-}$$

### 3.6 Coastal Features (ลักษณะชายฝั่ง)

Coastal processes (erosion, deposition) shape shorelines:

- **Erosional features**: Sea cliffs (ผาหิน), wave-cut platforms (พื้นผิวตัดคลื่น), sea caves, sea arches
- **Depositional features**: Beaches (หาด), spits, barrier islands, deltas (ดินดอนสามเหลี่ยม)
- **Thailand's coasts**: Gulf of Thailand (อ่าวไทย) has muddy coasts; Andaman Sea coast has rocky shores and coral reefs (แนวปะการัง)

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Tidal Range Calculation

> At a coastal station, the high tide reaches $4.2$ m and the low tide drops to $0.8$ m. Calculate the tidal range and classify the tide type.

**Solution:**

$$\text{Tidal range} = 4.2 - 0.8 = 3.4 \text{ m}$$

This is a **macrotidal** range ($>4$ m is macrotidal, $2$–$4$ m is mesotidal). At $3.4$ m, it is **mesotidal**.

### Type 2: Identifying Tide Type from Moon Phase

> The moon is at **full moon** phase. What type of tide will occur and why?

**Solution:** At full moon, the Sun, Earth, and Moon are aligned → **spring tide** (น้ำขึ้นสูงสุด). Gravitational forces reinforce each other, producing the highest high tides and lowest low tides.

### Type 3: Analyzing Ocean Current Effects

> The Gulf Stream carries warm water from the Gulf of Mexico to Western Europe. Explain its climatic effect.

**Solution:** The warm Gulf Stream (กระแสกัลฟ์สตรีม) transports heat poleward, making Western Europe (e.g., UK, Norway) significantly warmer than locations at the same latitude in North America. Without it, London would have a climate similar to northern Canada.

### Type 4: Salinity Calculation

> $500$ g of seawater is evaporated, leaving $17.5$ g of salt. What is the salinity?

**Solution:**

$$S = \frac{17.5}{500} \times 1000‰ = 35‰$$

This is the average ocean salinity.
