# Astrophysics and Cosmology — ฟิสิกส์ดาราศาสตร์และจักรวาลวิทยา

> *"The cosmos is within us. We are made of star-stuff. We are a way for the universe to know itself."* — Carl Sagan

Astrophysics (ฟิสิกส์ดาราศาสตร์) applies the laws of physics to understand stars, galaxies, and the universe at large. Cosmology (จักรวาลวิทยา) is the study of the universe as a whole — its origin, evolution, large-scale structure, and ultimate fate. Modern cosmology rests on two pillars: Einstein's general relativity and the observational discovery that the universe is expanding. The Big Bang theory, supported by the cosmic microwave background and the abundance of light elements, describes a universe that began hot and dense ~13.8 billion years ago.

In the ว303 curriculum, students learn about stellar properties and evolution (the H-R diagram), galaxies, Hubble's law, the Big Bang theory, the cosmic microwave background, and are briefly introduced to dark matter and dark energy — two of the greatest mysteries in modern physics.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Stellar Properties

A star's key observable properties include:

- **Luminosity (ความสว่าง) $L$:** total energy radiated per second, in watts. The Sun's luminosity is $L_\odot = 3.83 \times 10^{26}$ W.
- **Surface temperature:** determined from the star's spectrum and color. Measured in kelvin; related to peak wavelength by **Wien's law**: $\lambda_{\max} T = 2.898 \times 10^{-3}$ m·K.
- **Apparent brightness:** $b = L/(4\pi d^2)$ follows from inverse-square law.
- **Mass:** determines the star's life path. Measured in solar masses $M_\odot = 1.99 \times 10^{30}$ kg.

### 3.2 The H-R Diagram

The **Hertzsprung-Russell diagram** (ไดอะแกรมเอช-อาร์) plots **luminosity vs. surface temperature** (or absolute magnitude vs. spectral class). Key regions:

| Region | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Main sequence** | Stars fusing H→He in core; ~90% of stars | Sun |
| **Red giants** | Cool, luminous, large; post-main-sequence | Aldebaran, Arcturus |
| **Supergiants** | Very massive, very luminous | Betelgeuse, Rigel |
| **White dwarfs** | Hot, dim, small; stellar remnants | Sirius B |

The main sequence runs from hot-luminous (upper left) to cool-dim (lower right). A star's position depends primarily on its **mass**.

### 3.3 Stellar Evolution

A star's life cycle depends on its mass:

**Low-mass stars (0.08–8 $M_\odot$):**

$$\text{Molecular cloud} \to \text{Protostar} \to \text{Main sequence} \to \text{Red giant} \to \text{Planetary nebula} \to \text{White dwarf}$$

**High-mass stars (>8 $M_\odot$):**

$$\text{Main sequence} \to \text{Red supergiant} \to \text{Supernova} \to \text{Neutron star or Black hole}$$

Key stages:
- **Main sequence (ลำดับหลัก):** Hydrogen fusion via proton-proton chain or CNO cycle. The Sun has ~5 billion years remaining here.
- **Red giant:** Core hydrogen exhausted; helium and heavier element fusion in shells.
- **Supernova (ซูเปอร์โนวา):** Catastrophic collapse of massive star's core, releasing ~10⁴⁴ J and producing elements heavier than iron.
- **Neutron star (ดาวนิวตรอน):** Ultra-dense remnant ($\rho \sim 10^{17}$ kg/m³), radius ~10 km.
- **Black hole (หลุมดำ):** Collapse beyond neutron degeneracy pressure; event horizon at Schwarzschild radius $R_S = 2GM/c^2$.

### 3.4 Galaxies

Galaxies (ดาราจักร) are gravitationally bound systems of stars, gas, dust, and dark matter. Types:

- **Spiral (ดาราจักรชนิดก้นหอย):** flat disk with spiral arms; active star formation (Milky Way, Andromeda)
- **Elliptical:** smooth, ellipsoidal; mostly old stars
- **Irregular:** no regular structure; often result of galaxy collisions

The Milky Way (ทางช้างเผือก) contains ~100–400 billion stars and is part of the Local Group of galaxies.

### 3.5 Hubble's Law and Cosmic Expansion

Edwin Hubble (1929) discovered that distant galaxies recede from us with velocity proportional to distance:

$$v = H_0 \, d$$

where $H_0 \approx 70$ km/s/Mpc is **Hubble's constant** (ค่าคงที่ของฮับเบิล). This is measured via **redshift (แดงเลื่อน)** of galaxy spectra:

$$z = \frac{\lambda_{\text{observed}} - \lambda_{\text{emitted}}}{\lambda_{\text{emitted}}}$$

For $v \ll c$: $z \approx v/c$. Hubble's law is the primary evidence that the universe is **expanding**. It implies that all galaxies were once at the same point — the Big Bang.

### 3.6 The Big Bang Theory

The **Big Bang theory** (ทฤษฎีมหาวิถีปะทุกำเดิง) states that the universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since. Key evidence:

1. **Hubble expansion:** galaxies recede with $v \propto d$.
2. **Cosmic microwave background (CMB):** relic radiation at $T = 2.725$ K, discovered by Penzias and Wilson (1965).
3. **Light element abundance:** ~75% hydrogen, ~25% helium by mass — matches Big Bang nucleosynthesis predictions.

Timeline (simplified):

| Time after Big Bang | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 | Big Bang (singularity) |
| $10^{-36}$ s | Inflation (rapid expansion) |
| 3 min | Nucleosynthesis (H, He nuclei) |
| 380,000 yr | Recombination; CMB released |
| ~100 Myr | First stars form |
| ~1 Gyr | First galaxies |
| ~9.2 Gyr | Solar system forms |
| 13.8 Gyr | Present day |

### 3.7 Dark Matter and Dark Energy

Observations of galaxy rotation curves, gravitational lensing, and structure formation indicate that **~85% of matter is invisible** — this is **dark matter (สสารมืด)**. It does not interact electromagnetically but exerts gravitational force. Candidates include WIMPs and axions, but the nature remains unknown.

Since 1998, observations of distant supernovae show that the universe's expansion is **accelerating**. This is attributed to **dark energy (พลังงานมืด)**, a mysterious form of energy permeating space, making up ~68% of the universe's total energy content.

The composition of the universe:

$$\Omega_{\text{total}} \approx 100\% = \underbrace{5\%}_{\text{ordinary matter}} + \underbrace{27\%}_{\text{dark matter}} + \underbrace{68\%}_{\text{dark energy}}$$

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Hubble's law — distance and velocity
> A galaxy has a recession velocity of 14,000 km/s. Find its distance using $H_0 = 70$ km/s/Mpc.

**Solution:**

$$d = \frac{v}{H_0} = \frac{14000}{70} = 200 \ \text{Mpc}$$

In light-years: $200 \times 3.26 \approx 652$ million light-years.

---

### Type 2: Wien's law for a star
> A star has a surface temperature of 10,000 K. Find its peak emission wavelength.

**Solution:**

$$\lambda_{\max} = \frac{2.898 \times 10^{-3}}{T} = \frac{2.898 \times 10^{-3}}{10000} = 2.898 \times 10^{-7} \ \text{m} = 290 \ \text{nm}$$

This is in the ultraviolet — the star appears blue-white. ✓

---

### Type 3: Luminosity from brightness and distance
> A star has apparent brightness $b = 1.0 \times 10^{-10}$ W/m² and is at distance $d = 10$ pc ($3.086 \times 10^{17}$ m). Find its luminosity.

**Solution:**

$$L = 4\pi d^2 \, b = 4\pi (3.086 \times 10^{17})^2 (1.0 \times 10^{-10})$$

$$L \approx 4\pi \times 9.52 \times 10^{34} \times 10^{-10} \approx 1.20 \times 10^{26} \ \text{W}$$

This is about $0.31 \, L_\odot$ — a dim star slightly less luminous than the Sun. ✓

---

### Type 4: Redshift calculation
> A galaxy's Hα line (rest wavelength 656.3 nm) is observed at 680.5 nm. Find the redshift and recession velocity.

**Solution:**

$$z = \frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda_0} = \frac{680.5 - 656.3}{656.3} = \frac{24.2}{656.3} \approx 0.0369$$

For $z \ll 1$: $v \approx zc = 0.0369 \times 3 \times 10^5 \approx 11{,}070$ km/s.

Distance: $d = v/H_0 = 11070/70 \approx 158$ Mpc. ✓

---

### Type 5: Schwarzschild radius
> Find the Schwarzschild radius of a black hole with mass 10 solar masses.

**Solution:**

$$R_S = \frac{2GM}{c^2} = \frac{2 \times 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 10 \times 1.99 \times 10^{30}}{(3 \times 10^8)^2}$$

$$R_S \approx \frac{2.66 \times 10^{21}}{9 \times 10^{16}} \approx 29.5 \ \text{km}$$

The solar mass Schwarzschild radius is $\approx 2.95$ km. ✓
