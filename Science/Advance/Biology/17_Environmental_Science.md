# Environmental Science — วิทยาศาสตร์สิ่งแวดล้อม

> *"We do not inherit the earth from our ancestors; we borrow it from our children."* — Native American Proverb

Environmental science (วิทยาศาสตร์สิ่งแวดล้อม) integrates biology, chemistry, geology, and social science to understand the natural world and how human activities impact it. At its core is the concept of biodiversity (ความหลากหลายทางชีวภาพ), which encompasses the variety of life at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels. Human activities — deforestation, pollution, overexploitation, and climate change — are driving species to extinction at rates far exceeding natural background rates.

Sustainable development (การพัฒนาที่ยั่งยืน) seeks to meet present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs. This requires understanding ecological limits, managing natural resources responsibly, reducing pollution, and mitigating climate change (การเปลี่ยนแปลงสภาพภูมิอากาศ). Conservation biology draws on ecological principles to protect and restore biodiversity.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Biodiversity (ความหลากหลายทางชีวภาพ)

Biodiversity exists at three levels:
- **Genetic diversity** (ความหลากหลายทางพันธุกรรม): variation in genes within a population, enabling adaptation
- **Species diversity** (ความหลากหลายของชนิดพันธุ์): the number and relative abundance of species in a community
- **Ecosystem diversity** (ความหลากหลายของระบบนิเวศ): the variety of ecosystems in a region

Biodiversity provides **ecosystem services** (บริการจากระบบนิเวศ): provisioning (food, medicine), regulating (climate, water purification), supporting (nutrient cycling), and cultural (recreation, spiritual).

### 3.2 Threats to Biodiversity (ภัยคุกคามต่อความหลากหลายทางชีวภาพ)

- **Habitat destruction** (การทำลายถิ่นที่อยู่): deforestation, urbanization, agriculture
- **Pollution** (มลพิษ): chemical pollutants contaminate air, water, and soil
- **Overexploitation** (การใช้ทรัพยากรเกินควร): overfishing, overhunting
- **Invasive species** (สิ่งมีชีวิตต่างถิ่น): outcompete native species (e.g., water hyacinth, golden apple snail in Thailand)
- **Climate change** (การเปลี่ยนแปลงสภาพภูมิอากาศ): shifts in temperature and precipitation patterns

### 3.3 Climate Change and the Greenhouse Effect

Greenhouse gases (ก๊าซเรือนกระจก) — CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs — trap infrared radiation, warming Earth's surface. Human activities (fossil fuel combustion, deforestation, agriculture) have increased atmospheric CO₂ from ~280 ppm (pre-industrial) to over 420 ppm. Consequences include rising sea levels, extreme weather, ocean acidification, and ecosystem disruption.

### 3.4 Pollution Types

- **Air pollution** (มลพิษทางอากาศ): SO₂ and NOₓ cause acid rain (ฝนกรด); particulate matter affects respiratory health; ozone depletion from CFCs
- **Water pollution** (มลพิษทางน้ำ): eutrophication (eutrophication, ยูโทรฟิเคชัน) from excess nutrients causes algal blooms and dead zones; heavy metals (Hg, Pb, Cd) bioaccumulate
- **Soil pollution** (มลพิษทางดิน): pesticides, heavy metals, industrial waste reduce soil fertility and contaminate food chains

### 3.5 Conservation Strategies

- **In-situ conservation** (การอนุรักษ์ในถิ่น): national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, marine protected areas
- **Ex-situ conservation** (การอนุรักษ์นอกถิ่น): zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, gene banks
- **Legislation**: CITES, Endangered Species Act, Thai Wildlife Conservation Act (พ.ร.บ.สงวนและคุ้มครองสัตว์ป่า)
- **Sustainable practices**: sustainable agriculture, eco-tourism, REDD+ (reducing emissions from deforestation)

### 3.6 Sustainable Development (การพัฒนาที่ยั่งยืน)

The concept balances economic growth, social equity, and environmental protection. Key approaches include renewable energy, circular economy (เศรษฐกิจหมุนเวียน), carbon footprint reduction (ลดรอยเท้าคาร์บอน), and the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Biodiversity Index Calculation
> A forest has 5 species with abundances: 40, 30, 15, 10, 5. Calculate the Shannon-Wiener diversity index.

**Solution:**
1. Total N = 40 + 30 + 15 + 10 + 5 = 100
2. Calculate proportions: p₁ = 0.40, p₂ = 0.30, p₃ = 0.15, p₄ = 0.10, p₅ = 0.05
3. $H' = -\sum p_i \ln p_i = -(0.40\ln0.40 + 0.30\ln0.30 + 0.15\ln0.15 + 0.10\ln0.10 + 0.05\ln0.05)$
4. $H' = -(−0.366 − 0.361 − 0.284 − 0.230 − 0.150) = 1.39$

### Type 2: Greenhouse Effect Analysis
> Explain why methane (CH₄) is considered a more potent greenhouse gas than CO₂ per molecule.

**Solution:**
1. CH₄ has a Global Warming Potential (GWP) ~28–36 times that of CO₂ over 100 years
2. CH₄ absorbs more infrared radiation per molecule due to its molecular vibration modes
3. Although CH₄ has a shorter atmospheric lifetime (~12 years vs centuries for CO₂), its per-molecule warming effect is much greater
4. Major sources: livestock, rice paddies, landfills, natural gas leaks
