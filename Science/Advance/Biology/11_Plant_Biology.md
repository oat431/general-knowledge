# Plant Biology — ชีววิทยาของพืช

> *"The clearest way into the Universe is through a forest wilderness."* — John Muir

Plant biology (ชีววิทยาของพืช) covers the structure, function, and reproduction of plants — the primary producers (ผู้ผลิต) of nearly all terrestrial ecosystems. From the cellular organization of tissues (เนื้อเยื่อ) to the complex physiology of photosynthesis (การสังเคราะห์ด้วยแสง), transpiration (การคายน้ำ), and reproduction (การสืบพันธุ์), plants demonstrate remarkable adaptations to life on land.

Plants evolved from green algae and developed key innovations: cuticles (คิวติเคิล) to prevent water loss, stomata (ปากใบ) for gas exchange, vascular tissue (เนื้อเยื่อลำเลียง) for transport, and seeds (เมล็ด) for embryo protection. Understanding plant biology is essential for agriculture (เกษตรกรรม), ecology, and addressing food security challenges.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Plant Tissues

**Meristematic tissue** (เนื้อเยื่อเจริญ) consists of actively dividing cells found at growth regions: **apical meristems** (ปลายยอด/ปลายราก) for length growth, **lateral meristems** (ข้างลำต้น) for width growth, and **intercalary meristems** (ระหว่างข้อ) in grasses.

**Permanent tissues** (เนื้อเยื่อถาวร) arise from meristematic differentiation: **Epidermis** (เอพิเดอร์มิส) — outer protective layer, often with waxy cuticle. **Vascular tissue** — xylem (ไซเลม, dead cells transporting water and minerals upward) and phloem (โฟลเอ็ม, living cells transporting sugars bidirectionally). **Ground tissue** — parenchyma (พารังคิมา, thin-walled, storage/photosynthesis), collenchyma (คอลเลงคิมา, flexible support), and sclerenchyma (สเคลเรงคิมา, rigid, thick-walled support).

### 3.2 Plant Organs: Root, Stem, Leaf

**Root** (ราก): anchor plant, absorb water/minerals. Structure: epidermis → cortex → endodermis (with Casparian strip) → vascular cylinder (stele). Root hairs increase surface area. **Stem** (ลำต้น): support, transport between roots and leaves. Vascular bundles arranged differently in monocots (scattered) vs. dicots (ring). **Leaf** (ใบ): primary photosynthetic organ. Structure: upper/lower epidermis, mesophyll (palisade and spongy layers with chloroplasts), vascular bundles (veins), stomata with guard cells.

### 3.3 Photosynthesis

The overall equation:

$$6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$$

**Light-dependent reactions** (ปฏิกิริยาที่ต้องใช้แสง) occur in the **thylakoid membranes** (เยื่อไทลาคอยด์). Photosystem II (PSII) absorbs light at 680 nm, splits water (photolysis — การสลายตัวของน้ำด้วยแสง), releasing O₂. Electrons pass through the **electron transport chain** (สายพานอิเล็กตรอน) to Photosystem I (PSI, absorbs at 700 nm). NADP⁺ is reduced to **NADPH**. The proton gradient drives **ATP synthase** (ATP ซินเทส) to produce **ATP** via chemiosmosis (เคมีออสโมซิส).

**Calvin cycle** (วงจรแคลวิน) occurs in the **stroma** (สโตรา). **Carbon fixation** (การตรึงคาร์บอน): CO₂ is attached to RuBP by **RuBisCO** enzyme → 3-PGA. **Reduction**: ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA to G3P. **Regeneration**: some G3P regenerates RuBP; the rest is used to build glucose. For every 3 CO₂ fixed: 6 ATP and 3 NADPH consumed → 1 G3P (net).

### 3.4 Transpiration and Water Transport

**Transpiration** (การคายน้ำ) is the evaporation of water from leaves through stomata, driven by humidity gradient. The **cohesion-tension theory** (ทฤษฎีแรงยึดเหนี่ยว-แรงดึง) explains water movement: evaporation creates tension (negative pressure) in leaf xylem → cohesive water molecules form a continuous column → pulls water upward from roots. Root pressure (แรงดันราก) provides additional push.

### 3.5 Tropisms

**Tropisms** (ทรอพิซึม) are directional growth responses toward or away from stimuli. **Phototropism** (การเอียงเข้าหาแสง) — stems grow toward light, mediated by **auxin** (ออกซิน) redistribution. **Gravitropism** (การตอบสนองต่อแรงโน้มถ่วง) — roots grow downward (positive gravitropism), stems grow upward (negative gravitropism), mediated by **statoliths** (สตาโทลิธ, starch-filled amyloplasts). Other plant hormones: **gibberellins** (จิบเบอเรลลิน, stem elongation), **cytokinins** (ไซโตไคนิน, cell division), **ethylene** (เอทิลีน, fruit ripening), **abscisic acid** (ABA, stress response, stomatal closure).

### 3.6 Flowering Plant Reproduction

A flower (ดอกไม้) contains: **sepals** (กลีบเลี้ยง), **petals** (กลีบดอก), **stamens** (เกสรตัวผู้ = anther + filament), and **carpel/pistil** (เกสรตัวเมีย = stigma + style + ovary).

**Pollination** (การถ่ายละอองเรณู): transfer of pollen from anther to stigma. **Self-pollination** (การผสมตัวเอง) vs. **cross-pollination** (การผสมข้าม) by wind, insects, birds, or water. **Fertilization** (การปฏิสนธิ): pollen tube grows down style; double fertilization in angiosperms — one sperm + egg → **zygote** (2n), one sperm + polar nuclei → **endosperm** (3n, เอนโดสเพิร์ม). After fertilization: ovule → **seed** (เมล็ด), ovary → **fruit** (ผล). Seed dispersal (การกระจายเมล็ด) by wind, water, animals, or mechanical means.

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Photosynthesis Energy Calculations
> How many ATP and NADPH molecules are needed to produce one molecule of glucose via the Calvin cycle?

**Solution:**
1. One glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) requires fixing 6 CO₂ (two turns of the full cycle producing one G3P each; 6 G3P needed, 5 recycled)
2. Per 3 CO₂ fixed: 9 ATP and 6 NADPH
3. For 6 CO₂: **18 ATP and 12 NADPH**

### Type 2: Identifying Tissue Types
> A plant cross-section shows thick-walled cells with lignin deposits and no living contents at maturity. Identify the tissue.

**Solution:** This is **sclerenchyma** (สเคลเรงคิมา) — permanent ground tissue with thick, lignified secondary walls providing structural support. Examples: fibers and sclereids (stone cells in pears).

### Type 3: Tropism Experiment Design
> Design an experiment to demonstrate that auxin causes phototropism in coleoptiles.

**Solution:**
1. **Control**: intact coleoptile with unilateral light → bends toward light
2. **Treatment 1**: tip removed → no bending → tip is necessary
3. **Treatment 2**: tip removed, agar block with auxin placed asymmetrically → bends → auxin causes bending
4. **Treatment 3**: tip removed, agar block with auxin centered → grows straight upward → asymmetry of auxin causes directional bending
5. **Conclusion**: Auxin redistribution from the light side to the shaded side causes differential cell elongation and bending toward light.
