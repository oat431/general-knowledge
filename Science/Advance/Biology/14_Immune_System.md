# Immune System — ระบบภูมิคุ้มกัน

> *"The body's ability to defend itself against foreign invaders is one of the most remarkable feats of biological engineering."* — Charles Janeway

The immune system (ระบบภูมิคุ้มกัน) is a complex network of cells, tissues, and organs that work together to defend the body against pathogens including bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites. It comprises two main branches: innate immunity (ภูมิคุ้มกันแต่กำเนิด) which provides immediate, non-specific defense, and adaptive immunity (ภูมิคุ้มกันจำเพาะ) which mounts targeted responses and creates immunological memory. Understanding how these systems interact is essential for medicine, vaccination, and treating immune disorders.

The adaptive immune system relies on lymphocytes — B cells (เซลล์บี) that produce antibodies and T cells (เซลล์ที) that directly attack infected cells or coordinate immune responses. The interplay between these cell types, along with antigen-presenting cells, forms the basis of the primary and secondary immune responses that underpin vaccination strategies.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Innate Immunity (ภูมิคุ้มกันแต่กำเนิด)

Innate immunity is the body's first line of defense, providing immediate but non-specific protection. It includes **physical barriers** such as skin (ผิวหนัง), mucous membranes (เยื่อเมือก), and secretions like tears and stomach acid. **Chemical barriers** include lysozyme in tears and low pH in the stomach.

When pathogens breach physical barriers, the **inflammatory response** (การอักเสบ) is triggered. Mast cells release histamine (ฮีสตามีน), causing vasodilation, increased permeability, and recruitment of phagocytes. **Phagocytes** (ฟาโกไซต์) such as neutrophils and macrophages engulf and destroy invaders through phagocytosis. **Natural killer (NK) cells** destroy virus-infected cells and tumor cells.

### 3.2 Adaptive Immunity (ภูมิคุ้มกันจำเพาะ)

Adaptive immunity is slower to activate but highly specific and generates **immunological memory**. It has two branches:

- **Humoral immunity** (ภูมิคุ้มกันแบบใช้น้ำเหลือง): mediated by B cells that differentiate into plasma cells (เซลล์พลาสมา) producing antibodies. Antibodies (IgG, IgM, IgA, IgI, IgE) bind to specific antigens, neutralizing pathogens or marking them for destruction.
- **Cell-mediated immunity** (ภูมิคุ้มกันแบบเซลล์): mediated by T cells. **Helper T cells** (Th) activate B cells and other immune cells. **Cytotoxic T cells** (Tc) directly kill infected cells by releasing perforin and granzymes.

### 3.3 Primary and Secondary Immune Response

The **primary response** occurs on first exposure to an antigen — it is slow (7–14 days), produces mainly IgM, and generates **memory B and T cells**. The **secondary response** upon re-exposure is faster (1–3 days), stronger, and produces mainly IgG. This is the principle behind vaccination (การให้วัคซีน).

### 3.4 Immune Disorders

- **Autoimmune diseases** (โรคภูมิแพ้ตนเอง): immune system attacks the body's own tissues (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, type 1 diabetes)
- **Allergies** (โรคภูมิแพ้): overreaction to harmless antigens (allergens) involving IgE and mast cell degranulation
- **HIV/AIDS**: HIV destroys helper T cells (CD4+), leading to immunodeficiency and susceptibility to opportunistic infections

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Comparing Primary and Secondary Immune Response
> A person is vaccinated against measles at age 1 and again at age 5. Compare the immune response after each vaccination.

**Solution:**
1. First vaccination (primary response): Slow onset (7–14 days), lower antibody titer, predominantly IgM, memory cells generated
2. Second vaccination (secondary response): Rapid onset (1–3 days), higher antibody titer, predominantly IgG, faster and stronger due to memory cells
3. The booster dose amplifies the memory pool, providing longer-lasting protection

### Type 2: Antibody-Antigen Interaction
> Explain how antibodies neutralize a bacterial toxin.

**Solution:**
1. Antibodies have variable regions that bind specifically to the toxin's epitopes (antigenic determinants)
2. Binding neutralizes the toxin by blocking its active site
3. Antibody-toxin complexes (immune complexes) are phagocytosed by macrophages
4. Complement activation may also be triggered, enhancing pathogen destruction
