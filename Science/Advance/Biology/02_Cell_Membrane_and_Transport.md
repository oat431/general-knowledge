# Cell Membrane and Transport — เยื่อหุ้มเซลล์และการลำเลียง

> *"The membrane is not a static wall but a dynamic, fluid structure."* — S.J. Singer & G.L. Nicolson (1972)

The cell membrane (เยื่อหุ้มเซลล์) is a selectively permeable barrier that controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell. According to the fluid mosaic model (แบบจำลองฟลูอิดโมเสก), the membrane consists of a phospholipid bilayer (ชั้นฟอสโฟลิปิด) in which proteins are embedded and move laterally. This dynamic structure allows the cell to maintain homeostasis (สภาวะสมดุล) by regulating ion concentrations, nutrient uptake, and waste removal.

Membrane transport mechanisms range from passive processes that require no energy input — such as simple diffusion (การแพร่), osmosis (ออสโมซิส), and facilitated diffusion (การแพร่แบบช่วยเหลือ) — to active transport (การลำเลียงแบบใช้พลังงาน) processes that consume ATP. Bulk transport methods including endocytosis (เอนโดไซโทซิส) and exocytosis (เอ็กโซไซโทซิส) move large molecules across the membrane.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 The Fluid Mosaic Model (แบบจำลองฟลูอิดโมเสก)

The membrane is composed of:
- **Phospholipid bilayer (ชั้นฟอสโฟลิปิด):** Each phospholipid has a hydrophilic (ชอบน้ำ) phosphate head and two hydrophobic (ไม่ชอบน้ำ) fatty acid tails. In water, they spontaneously form a bilayer with heads facing outward toward aqueous environments and tails facing inward.
- **Proteins (โปรตีน):** Integral proteins (โปรตีนภายใน) span the entire membrane; peripheral proteins (โปรตีนรอบนอก) attach to the surface. Functions include transport (channel and carrier proteins), enzymatic activity, signal transduction (receptors), and cell recognition (glycoproteins).
- **Cholesterol (คอเลสเตอรอล):** Embedded between phospholipids in animal cells; regulates membrane fluidity — prevents excessive fluidity at high temperatures and stiffening at low temperatures.
- **Carbohydrates (คาร์โบไฮเดรต):** Attached to proteins (glycoproteins) or lipids (glycolipids) on the extracellular surface; function in cell recognition and signaling.

### 3.2 Passive Transport (การลำเลียงแบบไม่ใช้พลังงาน)

Passive transport moves substances down their concentration gradient (เกรเดียนต์ความเข้มข้น) without energy expenditure.

**Simple Diffusion (การแพร่แบบธรรมดา):** Small, nonpolar molecules ($\ce{O2}$, $\ce{CO2}$, $\ce{N2}$) and small uncharged polar molecules (water, ethanol) pass directly through the phospholipid bilayer. Rate depends on concentration gradient, temperature, membrane thickness, and surface area.

**Osmosis (ออสโมซิส):** The net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration (higher water potential) to higher solute concentration (lower water potential).

- **Hypertonic (สารละลายไฮเพอร์โทนิก):** Higher solute outside -> water leaves -> cell shrinks (crenation in animal cells; plasmolysis in plant cells).
- **Hypotonic (สารละลายไฮโปโทนิก):** Lower solute outside -> water enters -> cell swells (lysis risk in animal cells; turgor pressure in plant cells).
- **Isotonic (สารละลายไอโซโทนิก):** Equal solute -> no net water movement.

**Facilitated Diffusion (การแพร่แบบช่วยเหลือ):** Polar or charged molecules that cannot cross the lipid bilayer use membrane proteins:
- **Channel proteins (โปรตีนช่อง):** Form hydrophilic pores; include ion channels (some gated by voltage, ligand, or mechanical stimulus).
- **Carrier proteins (โปรตีนบรรทุก):** Bind specific solutes and change shape to shuttle them across (e.g., glucose transporter GLUT).

### 3.3 Active Transport (การลำเลียงแบบใช้พลังงาน)

Moves substances against their concentration gradient, requiring ATP.

**Sodium-Potassium Pump (ปั๊มโซเดียม-โพแทสเซียม):** The most important active transporter in animal cells. Pumps 3 $\ce{Na+}$ out and 2 $\ce{K+}$ in per ATP hydrolyzed. This maintains the electrochemical gradient (เกรเดียนต์ไฟฟ้าเคมี) essential for nerve impulse transmission and muscle contraction.

**Proton Pump (ปั๊มโปรตอน):** Pumps $\ce{H+}$ ions across membranes; found in plant cell membranes, lysosomes, and mitochondrial cristae.

**Cotransport (การลำเลียงร่วม):** Uses the gradient established by primary active transport to move a second substance. Symport moves both in the same direction; antiport moves them in opposite directions.

### 3.4 Bulk Transport (การลำเลียงขนาดใหญ่)

- **Endocytosis (เอนโดไซโทซิส):** Membrane invaginates to engulf material.
  - *Phagocytosis (ฟาโกไซโทซิส):* Engulfs solid particles (e.g., white blood cells engulf bacteria).
  - *Pinocytosis (พิโนไซโทซิส):* Engulfs fluid and dissolved solutes.
  - *Receptor-mediated endocytosis:* Specific ligands bind receptors in coated pits (clathrin-coated).
- **Exocytosis (เอ็กโซไซโทซิส):** Intracellular vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane, releasing contents outside (e.g., neurotransmitter release, hormone secretion).

### 3.5 Turgor Pressure (แรงดันทอร์กอร์)

In plant cells, water entering the central vacuole creates outward pressure against the cell wall. This turgor pressure maintains plant rigidity. When a plant cell is placed in a hypertonic solution, water leaves the vacuole, the membrane pulls away from the wall, and **plasmolysis (พลาสโมไลซิส)** occurs. In contrast, the cell wall prevents lysis in hypotonic solutions.

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Predicting Cell Response to Solutions
> A plant cell is placed in a 0.5 M sucrose solution. The cell's internal solute concentration is 0.3 M. Predict what happens.

**Solution:** The external solution (0.5 M) is hypertonic relative to the cell (0.3 M). Water moves out of the cell by osmosis. The vacuole shrinks, the plasma membrane pulls away from the cell wall — **plasmolysis (พลาสโมไลซิส)** occurs. The cell becomes flaccid.

### Type 2: Transport Mechanism Identification
> Glucose enters a red blood cell rapidly even when blood glucose is low. What mechanism is responsible?

**Solution:** Glucose is a large polar molecule that cannot diffuse through the lipid bilayer. It enters via **facilitated diffusion (การแพร่แบบช่วยเหลือ)** through GLUT transporters (carrier proteins). This is passive — no ATP required — and the rate is limited by the number of transporter proteins (saturation kinetics).

### Type 3: Water Potential Calculation
> A cell has a solute potential ($\psi_s$) of -0.5 MPa and a pressure potential ($\psi_p$) of 0.3 MPa. What is the water potential? Will water enter or leave in pure water ($\psi = 0$)?

**Solution:**
$$\psi = \psi_s + \psi_p = -0.5 + 0.3 = -0.2 \text{ MPa}$$
Since the cell's water potential (-0.2 MPa) is lower than pure water (0 MPa), water will **enter** the cell by osmosis.
