# Biotechnology — เทคโนโลยีชีวภาพ

> *"The advance of technology is based on fitting in so that you don't even notice it."* — Harold Evans

Biotechnology (เทคโนโลยีชีวภาพ) is the application of biological systems, living organisms, or their derivatives to develop products and processes for specific use. Modern biotechnology centers on **recombinant DNA technology** (เทคโนโลยีดีเอ็นเอ recombinant), which allows scientists to combine genetic material from different sources. Key tools include restriction enzymes (เอนไซม์ตัดจำเพาะ), vectors (เวกเตอร์), PCR (ปฏิกิริยาลูกโซ่พอลิเมอเรส), and gel electrophoresis (อิเล็กโทรโฟรีซิส).

Applications range from producing human insulin in bacteria to creating genetically modified organisms (GMOs), cloning animals, gene therapy (การรักษาด้วยยีน), and the revolutionary CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing system (ระบบตัดต่อยีน CRISPR-Cas9). These technologies hold enormous promise for medicine, agriculture, and industry, but also raise important ethical questions.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Recombinant DNA Technology (เทคโนโลยีดีเอ็นเอ recombinant)

The process of combining DNA from different organisms:
1. **Isolate** the gene of interest and a vector (typically a plasmid)
2. **Cut** both with the same **restriction enzyme** — creates sticky ends or blunt ends
3. **Ligate** (join) the gene into the vector using **DNA ligase** (DNA ไลเกส)
4. **Transform** the recombinant vector into a host cell (e.g., E. coli)
5. **Select** and screen for successful transformants

Restriction enzymes (e.g., EcoRI cuts at GAATTC) recognize palindromic sequences and create complementary sticky ends that facilitate ligation.

### 3.2 PCR (ปฏิกิริยาลูกโซ่พอลิเมอเรส)

PCR amplifies a specific DNA segment through repeated cycles:
1. **Denaturation** (94–95°C): double-stranded DNA separates
2. **Annealing** (50–65°C): primers bind to complementary sequences
3. **Extension** (72°C): Taq polymerase synthesizes new strands

Each cycle doubles the target DNA; after 30 cycles, ~1 billion copies are produced. PCR is used in forensics, diagnostics, and research.

### 3.3 Gel Electrophoresis (อิเล็กโทรโฟรีซิส)

DNA fragments are separated by size through an agarose gel under an electric field. Smaller fragments migrate faster and farther. A DNA ladder (size standard) is used for comparison. The gel is stained with ethidium bromide or SYBR Green and visualized under UV light.

### 3.4 GMOs and Transgenic Organisms

**Genetically modified organisms** have had their DNA altered by adding genes from other species. Examples include:
- **Agriculture**: Bt corn (insect resistance), Roundup Ready soybeans (herbicide tolerance), Golden Rice (vitamin A)
- **Medicine**: bacteria producing human insulin, growth hormone, and clotting factors
- **Industry**: enzymes for detergents, biofuels

### 3.5 Gene Therapy (การรักษาด้วยยีน)

Gene therapy aims to treat genetic disorders by introducing functional genes into patient cells. Approaches include:
- **Ex-vivo**: cells removed, modified, and returned to patient
- **In-vivo**: therapeutic gene delivered directly into the body
- Vectors: viral vectors (retrovirus, adenovirus, AAV) or non-viral methods (liposomes, nanoparticles)

### 3.6 Cloning (การโคลนนิง)

- **Reproductive cloning**: creating a genetically identical organism (e.g., Dolly the sheep via somatic cell nuclear transfer — SCNT)
- **Therapeutic cloning**: creating embryonic stem cells for medical use without creating a full organism

### 3.7 Stem Cells (เซลล์ต้นกำเนิด)

Stem cells are undifferentiated cells capable of self-renewal and differentiation:
- **Embryonic stem cells** (ESC): pluripotent, can become any cell type
- **Adult stem cells**: multipotent, limited differentiation potential
- **Induced pluripotent stem cells** (iPSC): adult cells reprogrammed to pluripotency (Yamanaka factors)

### 3.8 CRISPR-Cas9 (ระบบตัดต่อยีน CRISPR-Cas9)

CRISPR-Cas9 is a revolutionary gene editing tool derived from bacterial immune systems:
1. A **guide RNA** (gRNA) directs the Cas9 nuclease to a specific DNA sequence
2. Cas9 creates a **double-strand break** at the target site
3. The cell's repair machinery fixes the break — either by NHEJ (disrupting the gene) or HDR (inserting a new sequence)

Applications: gene knockout, gene correction, disease models, and potential cures for genetic diseases.

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Restriction Enzyme Analysis
> A circular plasmid is cut with EcoRI (recognition site: GAATTC). If the plasmid has 3 EcoRI sites, how many fragments are produced?

**Solution:**
1. A circular DNA with n restriction sites produces n fragments when cut
2. 3 EcoRI sites → 3 fragments
3. Fragment sizes can be determined by gel electrophoresis comparison with a DNA ladder

### Type 2: PCR Problem
> Starting with 1 copy of DNA, how many copies are present after 20 PCR cycles?

**Solution:**
1. PCR amplification follows: copies = $2^n$ where n = number of cycles
2. After 20 cycles: $2^{20} = 1{,}048{,}576$ copies
3. Approximately 1 million copies from a single starting molecule
