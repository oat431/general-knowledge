# Human Reproduction and Development — ระบบสืบพันธุ์และการเจริญเติบโตของมนุษย์

> *"Recreation is not the chief end of man, but development is."* — Maria Montessori (adapted)

Human reproduction (การสืบพันธุ์ของมนุษย์) involves the production of gametes through meiosis, fertilization of the ovum by sperm, and the subsequent embryonic and fetal development within the uterus. The male and female reproductive systems (ระบบสืบพันธุ์เพศชายและเพศหญิง) are structurally different but functionally complementary, producing haploid gametes that unite to form a diploid zygote.

Development from zygote to birth involves cell division, differentiation, organogenesis, and the formation of supporting structures such as the placenta (รก). The hormonal regulation of the menstrual cycle (รอบเดือน), pregnancy, and lactation involves complex feedback loops among the hypothalamus, pituitary gland, and gonads.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Male Reproductive System (ระบบสืบพันธุ์เพศชาย)

The male reproductive system includes the **testes** (อัณฑะ) where spermatogenesis occurs, the **epididymis** (ท่อเก็บอสุจิ) for sperm maturation, the **vas deferens** (ท่อนำอสุจิ) for transport, and the **penis** (องคชาต) for delivery. Seminal vesicles, prostate gland, and bulbourethral gland contribute to **semen** (น้ำอสุจิ).

**Spermatogenesis** occurs in the seminiferous tubules. Spermatogonia (2n) undergo mitosis, then meiosis I and II to produce four haploid spermatids, which mature into spermatozoa. This process is continuous from puberty and is regulated by FSH and testosterone.

### 3.2 Female Reproductive System (ระบบสืบพันธุ์เพศหญิง)

The female system includes the **ovaries** (รังไข่), **fallopian tubes** (ท่อนำไข่/ปีกมดลูก), **uterus** (มดลูก), and **vagina** (ช่องคลอด). **Oogenesis** begins before birth — oogonia enter meiosis I but arrest at prophase I. Only after puberty does one oocyte complete meiosis I each cycle to form a secondary oocyte and first polar body.

### 3.3 Menstrual Cycle (รอบเดือน)

The ~28-day cycle is regulated by hypothalamic GnRH → pituitary FSH and LH → ovarian estrogen and progesterone:

- **Follicular phase** (ระยะฟอลลิคูลาร์, days 1–13): FSH stimulates follicle growth, estrogen rises
- **Ovulation** (การตกไข่, day 14): LH surge triggers release of secondary oocyte
- **Luteal phase** (ระยะลูทีล, days 15–28): Corpus luteum produces progesterone; if no implantation, hormone levels drop and menstruation occurs

### 3.4 Fertilization and Early Development

Fertilization typically occurs in the fallopian tube. Sperm penetrate the corona radiata and zona pellucida; one sperm fuses with the oocyte, triggering the cortical reaction to prevent polyspermy. The resulting **zygote** (ไซโกต) undergoes cleavage divisions → **morula** → **blastocyst** (บลาสโตซิสต์) → implantation in the uterine endometrium around day 7.

### 3.5 Embryonic and Fetal Development

- **Week 1–2**: Implantation, gastrulation (formation of three germ layers)
- **Week 3–8** (embryonic period): Organogenesis — major organs and structures form
- **Week 9–40** (fetal period): Growth and maturation of organ systems

The **placenta** (รก) facilitates gas exchange, nutrient transfer, and waste removal. It also produces hCG, progesterone, and estrogen to maintain pregnancy. The **umbilical cord** (สายสะดือ) contains two arteries and one vein.

### 3.6 Contraception (การคุมกำเนิด)

Methods include barrier methods (condom, diaphragm), hormonal methods (oral contraceptives, IUD), surgical methods (vasectomy, tubal ligation), and behavioral methods (abstinence, rhythm method). Effectiveness varies — hormonal and surgical methods are most reliable.

### 3.7 Sexually Transmitted Diseases (โรคติดต่อทางเพศสัมพันธ์)

Common STDs include HIV/AIDS, gonorrhea (หนองใน), syphilis (ซิฟิลิส), chlamydia, herpes, and HPV. Prevention includes barrier methods, vaccination (HPV), and regular screening.

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Hormonal Regulation of the Menstrual Cycle
> Describe the hormonal changes during days 10–16 of a 28-day menstrual cycle.

**Solution:**
1. Day 10–13: Rising estrogen from the dominant follicle causes negative feedback on FSH (decreasing) but positive feedback on LH (rising)
2. Day 14: LH surge triggers ovulation — the secondary oocyte is released
3. Day 15–16: The ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, producing progesterone and estrogen; progesterone causes negative feedback on GnRH, FSH, and LH

### Type 2: Comparing Spermatogenesis and Oogenesis
> Compare the products and timing of spermatogenesis versus oogenesis.

**Solution:**
1. Spermatogenesis: produces 4 viable sperm per cycle, continuous from puberty, occurs in seminiferous tubules, takes ~74 days
2. Oogenesis: produces 1 viable egg + 3 polar bodies per cycle, begins before birth (arrests at prophase I), resumes cyclically from puberty, one oocyte matures per ~28-day cycle
3. Key difference: spermatogenesis is continuous; oogenesis is cyclic and limited in number
