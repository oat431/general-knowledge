# Ecology — นิเวศวิทยา

> *"In nature, nothing exists alone."* — Rachel Carson

Ecology (นิเวศวิทยา) is the scientific study of the interactions between organisms and their environment. It operates at multiple levels: individual organisms, populations (ประชากร), communities (ชุมชน), ecosystems (ระบบนิเวศ), and the biosphere (ชีวมณฑล). Energy flows through ecosystems while matter cycles within them, creating the fundamental patterns that sustain life on Earth.

At the ecosystem level, energy enters primarily through photosynthesis by producers (ผู้ผลิต) and flows through trophic levels (ระดับ trophic) via consumption. Matter, unlike energy, is recycled through biogeochemical cycles (วัฏจักรสารเคมีในระบบนิเวศ) including the water, carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus cycles. Population dynamics are governed by birth rates, death rates, immigration, and emigration, producing characteristic growth curves.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Ecosystem Components (ส่วนประกอบของระบบนิเวศ)

An ecosystem consists of **biotic components** (สิ่งมีชีวิต) — producers, consumers (primary, secondary, tertiary), and decomposers — and **abiotic components** (สิ่งไม่มีชีวิต) — sunlight, temperature, water, soil minerals, and pH. Producers (autotrophs) convert inorganic matter into organic matter through photosynthesis or chemosynthesis.

### 3.2 Energy Flow (การไหลของพลังงาน)

Energy enters ecosystems as sunlight, captured by producers through photosynthesis. **Net primary productivity** (NPP, พลังงานศักย์สุทธิ) = GPP − respiration. Energy transfers between trophic levels at roughly **10% efficiency** (Rule of Ten). This limits the number of trophic levels (typically 4–5).

**Ecological pyramids** (พีระมิดนิเวศ) represent:
- **Pyramid of numbers**: number of organisms at each level
- **Pyramid of biomass**: dry mass at each level
- **Pyramid of energy**: energy flow (always upright)

### 3.3 Food Chains and Food Webs

A **food chain** is a linear sequence of organisms through which energy flows (e.g., grass → rabbit → fox → eagle). A **food web** is a network of interconnected food chains showing the complexity of feeding relationships. Most organisms occupy multiple trophic levels.

### 3.4 Biogeochemical Cycles (วัฏจักรสารเคมีในระบบนิเวศ)

- **Water cycle**: evaporation → condensation → precipitation → runoff/infiltration
- **Carbon cycle**: CO₂ fixation by photosynthesis, release by respiration, combustion, and decomposition; fossil fuels as carbon reservoirs
- **Nitrogen cycle**: N₂ fixation (by Rhizobium and lightning) → nitrification (NH₃ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻) → assimilation → ammonification → denitrification (NO₃⁻ → N₂)
- **Phosphorus cycle**: weathering of rocks → absorption by plants → transfer through food chain → decomposition → sedimentation (no atmospheric phase)

### 3.5 Population Ecology (นิเวศวิทยาประชากร)

Population growth models include:
- **Exponential growth** (J-curve): $N_t = N_0 e^{rt}$ — unlimited resources
- **Logistic growth** (S-curve): $rac{dN}{dt} = rN\left(rac{K - N}{K}

Factors limiting population: **density-dependent** (competition, predation, disease) and **density-independent** (weather, natural disasters).

### 3.6 Community Ecology (นิเวศวิทยาชุมชน)

Species interactions include:
- **Symbiosis** (อยู่ร่วม): mutualism (+/+), commensalism (+/0), parasitism (+/−)
- **Competition** (−/−): interspecific and intraspecific
- **Predation** (+/−): predator-prey relationships with co-evolution

**Ecological succession** (การเปลี่ยนแปลงของชุมชน): primary (on bare substrate) and secondary (after disturbance). Climax community (ชุมชนไคลแมกซ์) is the stable endpoint.

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Energy Flow Calculation
> A grassland ecosystem has NPP of 20,000 kJ/m²/year. Calculate the energy available to secondary consumers.

**Solution:**
1. Producers (NPP) = 20,000 kJ/m²/year
2. Primary consumers receive ~10% = 2,000 kJ/m²/year
3. Secondary consumers receive ~10% of primary = 200 kJ/m²/year
4. Energy available to secondary consumers ≈ 200 kJ/m²/year

### Type 2: Population Growth
> A bacterial population starts with 100 individuals, grows at r = 0.5/hour. What is the population after 6 hours (assuming unlimited resources)?

**Solution:**
1. Use exponential growth: $N_t = N_0 e^{rt}$
2. $N_t = 100 \times e^{0.5 \times 6} = 100 \times e^3$
3. $N_t = 100 \times 20.09 ≈ 2{,}009$ bacteria
