# Particle Physics — ฟิสิกส์อนุภาค

> *"I learned very early the difference between knowing the name of something and knowing something."* — Richard Feynman

Particle physics (ฟิสิกส์อนุภาค) is the study of the fundamental constituents of matter and the forces that govern their interactions. As physicists probed deeper into the atom — past nuclei into the subnuclear realm — they discovered a bewildering "zoo" of particles. By the 1970s, these were unified into the elegant **Standard Model**, which describes all known matter in terms of a small set of fundamental particles and three of the four fundamental forces. The Higgs boson discovery in 2012 completed the model.

In the ว303 curriculum, students are introduced to the Standard Model at a conceptual level: fundamental particles (quarks and leptons), the four fundamental forces and their carriers, antimatter, and qualitative understanding of Feynman diagrams. The goal is conceptual literacy rather than advanced quantum field theory.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 The Four Fundamental Forces

| Force | Relative strength | Range | Carrier | Acts on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Strong** | 1 | ~1 fm (short) | Gluon (g) | Quarks, gluons |
| **Electromagnetic** | $10^{-2}$ | Infinite | Photon (γ) | Charged particles |
| **Weak** | $10^{-6}$ | ~0.001 fm (very short) | W⁺, W⁻, Z⁰ | All fermions |
| **Gravity** | $10^{-39}$ | Infinite | Graviton (theorized) | All mass/energy |

The strong force binds quarks inside hadrons and holds nuclei together (via residual strong force). The weak force is responsible for beta decay. The electromagnetic force governs chemistry and everyday phenomena. Gravity is negligible at the particle scale but dominates at astronomical scales.

### 3.2 Fermions: Quarks and Leptons

All matter particles are **fermions** (half-integer spin) and come in two families:

**Quarks (ควาร์ก)** — six "flavors" in three generations:

| Generation | Up-type (+2/3) | Down-type (−1/3) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | up (u) | down (d) |
| 2 | charm (c) | strange (s) |
| 3 | top (t) | bottom (b) |

Quarks are never found in isolation (confinement). They have fractional charges: +2/3 or −1/3 of the electron charge.

**Leptons (เลปตอน)** — six particles in three generations:

| Generation | Charged lepton | Neutrino |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | electron (e⁻) | electron neutrino (νₑ) |
| 2 | muon (μ⁻) | muon neutrino (ν_μ) |
| 3 | tau (τ⁻) | tau neutrino (ν_τ) |

Leptons have integer charges (0 or −1) and do not participate in the strong force.

### 3.3 Hadrons: Baryons and Mesons

Quarks combine into **hadrons (แฮดรอน)**:

- **Baryons (แบริออน):** three quarks (qqq). Examples: proton (uud), neutron (udd).
- **Mesons (เมซอน):** quark + antiquark (q$\bar{q}$). Examples: pion π⁺ (u$\bar{d}$), kaon K⁰ (d$\bar{s}$).

Baryons have baryon number $B = 1$; mesons have $B = 0$. Antibaryons have $B = -1$.

### 3.4 Antimatter

Every particle has a corresponding **antiparticle (ปฏิอนุภาค)** with the same mass but opposite charge and other quantum numbers:

- electron (e⁻) ↔ positron (e⁺)
- proton (p) ↔ antiproton ($\bar{p}$)
- neutrino (ν) ↔ antineutrino ($\bar{\nu}$)

When a particle meets its antiparticle, they **annihilate**, converting all mass to energy (typically photons). Conversely, **pair production** creates a particle-antiparticle pair from a sufficiently energetic photon ($E \geq 2mc^2$) near a nucleus for momentum conservation.

### 3.5 Conservation Laws

In particle reactions, several quantities are conserved:

- **Electric charge (Q):** always conserved
- **Baryon number (B):** quarks have $B = 1/3$, so baryons have $B = 1$
- **Lepton numbers ($L_e, L_\mu, L_\tau$):** each lepton family has its own conserved number
- **Energy and momentum:** always conserved
- **Strangeness:** conserved in strong and EM interactions, but **not** in weak interactions

### 3.6 Feynman Diagrams (Conceptual)

A **Feynman diagram (ไดอะแกรมเฟย์นแมน)** is a space-time picture of a particle interaction:

- **Time** flows left to right (or bottom to top, by convention).
- **Straight lines** represent fermions (matter particles).
- **Wavy lines** represent photons; **curly lines** represent gluons; **dashed lines** represent Higgs bosons.
- **Vertices** (intersection points) represent interactions — each vertex involves a force carrier.

Example: electron-electron scattering via photon exchange — two electrons approach, exchange a virtual photon, and scatter apart.

At the high school level, students read these diagrams qualitatively: identify incoming particles, exchanged force carriers, and outgoing products.

### 3.7 The Higgs Mechanism (Introduction)

The **Higgs boson (บอสอนฮิกส์)**, discovered at CERN in 2012, arises from the Higgs field that permeates all space. Particles acquire mass through interaction with this field — the stronger the coupling, the more massive the particle. Photons and gluons do not couple to the Higgs field and are therefore massless (gluons are massless in the Standard Model Lagrangian, though confinement complicates the picture).

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Quark content of hadrons
> Determine the quark content of a proton, neutron, and π⁺ pion.

**Solution:**

- **Proton:** charge +1 → uud: $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{3} = +1$ ✓
- **Neutron:** charge 0 → udd: $\frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{3} = 0$ ✓
- **π⁺ meson:** charge +1 → u$\bar{d}$: $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = +1$ ✓ (antiquark of down has charge +1/3)

---

### Type 2: Conservation law check
> Is this reaction allowed? $p + p \to p + p + p + \bar{p}$

**Solution:**

Check conserved quantities:

| Quantity | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Charge | +1 + 1 = +2 | +1 + 1 + 1 + (−1) = +2 ✓ |
| Baryon number | 1 + 1 = 2 | 1 + 1 + 1 + (−1) = 2 ✓ |
| Energy | Sufficient kinetic energy needed | Possible if $E_k \geq 2m_p c^2$ ✓ |

This is **pair production** of a proton-antiproton pair. The reaction is allowed if sufficient kinetic energy is provided. ✓

---

### Type 3: Beta decay in terms of quarks
> Express beta-minus decay of a neutron at the quark level.

**Solution:**

Neutron (udd) → proton (uud):

At the quark level, a down quark converts to an up quark:

$$d \to u + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$$

Full: $n (udd) \to p (uud) + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$ ✓

This interaction is mediated by a **W⁻ boson** (weak force).

---

### Type 4: Pair production threshold
> What minimum photon energy is needed to produce an electron-positron pair?

**Solution:**

$$E_{\min} = 2 m_e c^2 = 2 \times 0.511 \ \text{MeV} = 1.022 \ \text{MeV}$$

The corresponding wavelength:

$$\lambda = \frac{hc}{E} = \frac{1240 \ \text{eV·nm}}{1.022 \times 10^6 \ \text{eV}} \approx 1.21 \times 10^{-3} \ \text{nm} = 0.00121 \ \text{nm}$$

This is in the gamma-ray region. A nearby nucleus is needed to conserve momentum. ✓
