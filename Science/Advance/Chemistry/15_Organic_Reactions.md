# Organic Reactions — ปฏิกิริยาเคมีอินทรีย์

> *"The only way to learn organic chemistry is to do organic chemistry."* — Paula Yurkanis Bruice

Organic reactions (ปฏิกิริยาเคมีอินทรีย์) are classified into several types according to their mechanism and the structural change that occurs: substitution, addition, elimination, condensation, hydrolysis, combustion, and redox. This note also covers the basic mechanisms SN1, SN2, E1, and E2 that explain how these reactions proceed at the molecular level.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Types of Organic Reactions (ประเภทของปฏิกิริยาเคมีอินทรีย์)

#### (1) Substitution Reaction (Sₙ)
**An atom or group is replaced by another group.**

Example: $\ce{CH4 + Cl2 ->[UV] CH3Cl + HCl}$

#### (2) Addition Reaction
**A reagent adds across a C=C double bond or C≡C triple bond.**

$$\ce{CH2=CH2 + Br2 -> CH2Br-CH2Br}$$

#### (3) Elimination Reaction (E)
**Atoms are removed to form a double or triple bond.**

$$\ce{CH3-CH2-OH ->[H2SO4, 170°C] CH2=CH2 + H2O}$$

#### (4) Condensation
**Molecules combine with the loss of water or another small molecule.**

$$\ce{R-COOH + R'-OH <=> R-COOR' + H2O}$$

#### (5) Hydrolysis
**Reaction with water.**

$$\ce{CH3COOC2H5 + H2O <=> CH3COOH + C2H5OH}$$

#### (6) Combustion
**Complete combustion (การเผาไหม้สมบูรณ์).**

$$\ce{C_nH_{2n+2} + (\frac{3n+1}{2})O2 -> nCO2 + (n+1)H2O}$$

Example: $\ce{CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O}, \quad \Delta H = -890\ \text{kJ/mol}$

#### (7) Oxidation–Reduction
- **Oxidation:** gain of O or loss of H, e.g. 1° alcohol (แอลกอฮอล์ปฐมภูมิ) → aldehyde (แอลดีไฮด์).
- **Reduction:** loss of O or gain of H.

### 3.2 Basic Reaction Mechanisms (กลไกปฏิกิริยาเบื้องต้น)

#### **SN1 (Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular)**

**Two steps:**
1. Substrate ionizes → **carbocation** (intermediate).
2. Nucleophile attacks.

**Characteristics:** 3° > 2° > 1° substrate, polar protic solvent.

#### **SN2 (Substitution Nucleophilic Bimolecular)**

**One step:** the nucleophile attacks from the back as the leaving group departs (inversion of configuration, i.e. Walden inversion).

**Characteristics:** CH₃ > 1° > 2° substrate, polar aprotic solvent.

#### **E1 (Elimination Unimolecular)**

Similar to SN1, but a base (often the solvent) removes a β-H to form the double bond.

#### **E2 (Elimination Bimolecular)**

A base removes a β-H while the leaving group departs in a single concerted step, forming the double bond.

**Summary comparison:**

|| Mechanism | Substrate | Nucleophile/Base | Solvent | Steps |
||---|---|---|---|---|
|| SN1 | 3° | weak | polar protic | 2 |
|| SN2 | CH₃, 1° | strong | polar aprotic | 1 |
|| E1 | 3° | weak | polar protic | 2 |
|| E2 | any | strong | — | 1 |

### 3.3 Combustion Reactions (ปฏิกิริยาการเผาไหม้)

#### (a) Complete Combustion (สมบูรณ์)

$$\ce{C_nH_{2n+2} + (\frac{3n+1}{2})O2 -> nCO2 + (n+1)H2O} \quad \text{(alkane)}$$

#### (b) Incomplete Combustion (ไม่สมบูรณ์)

Produces CO or C (soot) when O₂ is insufficient.

### 3.4 Esterification (ปฏิกิริยาเอสเทอริฟิเคชัน)

**Fischer esterification:** carboxylic acid (กรดคาร์บอกซิลิก) + alcohol (แอลกอฮอล์) → ester + water (catalyzed by H⁺).

$$\ce{CH3COOH + C2H5OH <=>[H2SO4][\Delta] CH3COOC2H5 + H2O}$$

### 3.5 Saponification (ปฏิกิริยาสะพอนิฟิเคชัน)

**Ester + strong base → salt of a carboxylic acid (soap) + alcohol.**

$$\ce{R-COO-R' + NaOH -> R-COONa + R'-OH}$$

Example: $\ce{(C17H35COO)3C3H5 + 3NaOH -> 3C17H35COONa + C3H5(OH)3}$

(triglyceride (ไตรกลีเซอไรด์) + NaOH → soap (สบู่) + glycerol (กลีเซอรอล))

### 3.6 Polymerization Reactions (ปฏิกิริยาพอลิเมอไรเซชัน)

#### (a) Addition Polymerization
The monomer (โมโนเมอร์) contains a C=C double bond; the double bond opens and monomers link into a chain.

$$n\ce{CH2=CH2 ->[-] (-CH2-CH2-)_{n}} \text{ (polyethylene)}$$

#### (b) Condensation Polymerization
Monomers link together with the elimination of a small molecule such as water or HCl.

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Predicting the Product of an Addition Reaction

> $\ce{CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr -> ?}$ (Markovnikov)

**Solution:** the H of HBr adds to the C that already has more H atoms.

$$\ce{CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr -> CH3-CHBr-CH3}$$ (2-bromopropane)

### Type 2: Combustion — Balancing the Equation

> Burning 5 mol of propane — how many mol of O₂ are required?

**Equation:**

$$\ce{C3H8 + 5O2 -> 3CO2 + 4H2O}$$

**Solution:** 5 mol propane → 25 mol O₂.

### Type 3: Esterification

> 1 mol of butanoic acid + 1 mol of ethanol → how many grams of ester are formed?

**Equation:** $\ce{C3H7COOH + C2H5OH -> C3H7COOC2H5 + H2O}$

**Solution:** 1 mol of ester = 116 g.

### Type 4: Choosing the Right Mechanism

> Substrate: $\ce{(CH3)3C-Br}$, nucleophile: $\ce{CH3O^-}$ (strong base)

**Solution:** E2 dominates (3° substrate + strong base → E2 over SN2).
