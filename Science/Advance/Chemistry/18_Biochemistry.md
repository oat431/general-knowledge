# Biochemistry — ชีวเคมี

> *"Life is a relationship among molecules, not a property of any one molecule."* — Carl Woese

**Biochemistry (ชีวเคมี)** studies the molecules and chemical reactions that occur in living systems. The four main classes of biomolecules (สารชีวโมเลกุล) are carbohydrates (คาร์โบไฮเดรต), lipids (ลิพิด), proteins (โปรตีน), and nucleic acids (กรดนิวคลีอิก). Each class has its own characteristic structure, function, and metabolism. This note links organic chemistry to biology.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Carbohydrates (คาร์โบไฮเดรต)

**General formula:** $\ce{C_n(H2O)_m}$ (C, H, O in 1:2:1 ratio).

#### (a) Monosaccharides
- **Glucose ($\ce{C6H12O6}$):** blood sugar (aldohexose).
- **Fructose:** fruit sugar (ketohexose).
- **Galactose:** component of lactose.

In solution they exist mainly as cyclic (pyranose / furanose) structures.

#### (b) Disaccharides
A **glycosidic** bond ($\ce{C-O-C}$) joins two monosaccharides.

|| Name | Components | Source |
||---|---|---|
|| **Maltose** | glucose + glucose | malt (ข้าวมอลต์) |
|| **Sucrose** | glucose + fructose | sugarcane (อ้อย) |
|| **Lactose** | glucose + galactose | milk (น้ำนม) |

$$\ce{C12H22O11} \text{ (disaccharides)}$$

**Hydrolysis:** $\ce{C12H22O11 + H2O -> 2C6H12O6}$

#### (c) Polysaccharides
- **Starch:** $\ce{(C6H10O5)_n}$, α-1,4-glycosidic (plant storage).
- **Glycogen:** α-1,4 and α-1,6 (animal/human storage, in liver).
- **Cellulose:** β-1,4-glycosidic (plant cell-wall structure) — humans cannot digest it.

### 3.2 Lipids (ลิพิด)

**Properties:** insoluble in water, soluble in organic solvents (ether, chloroform).

#### (a) Fats and Oils (ไขมันและน้ำมัน)
**Triglyceride:** glycerol + 3 fatty acids.

$$\ce{C3H5(OH)3 + 3 R-COOH -> (R-COO)3C3H5 + 3H2O} \text{ (esterification)}$$

- **Saturated** (no C=C in the fatty acid): solid fats (butter, lard).
- **Unsaturated** (with C=C): liquid oils (soybean, olive oil).

#### (b) Phospholipids
glycerol + 2 fatty acids + 1 phosphate group → main component of cell membranes.

#### (c) Steroids
Four fused carbon rings (e.g. cholesterol, sex hormones).

#### (d) Waxes
Esters of a long-chain fatty acid and a long-chain alcohol.

### 3.3 Proteins (โปรตีน)

#### (a) Amino Acids
**General structure:**

$$\ce{H2N-CH(R)-COOH}$$

- R = side chain (20 types in nature).
- Both an $\ce{-NH2}$ group (basic) and an $\ce{-COOH}$ group (acidic) → exists as a **zwitterion** in water.

#### (b) Peptide Bond

$$\ce{H2N-CH(R1)-COOH + H2N-CH(R2)-COOH -> H2N-CH(R1)-CO-NH-CH(R2)-COOH + H2O}$$

The linkage: $\ce{-CO-NH-}$ (amide bond).

#### (c) Four Levels of Structure
- **Primary:** the linear sequence of amino acids.
- **Secondary:** α-helix / β-sheet (held by H-bonds).
- **Tertiary:** 3D folding.
- **Quaternary:** several polypeptide chains together (e.g. hemoglobin).

#### (d) Enzymes (เอนไซม์)
- Proteins that act as biological **catalysts**.
- **Specific** to their substrate.
- **Active site** binds the substrate (lock-and-key / induced fit).
- Activity is affected by pH, temperature, and substrate concentration.

$$\ce{E + S <=> ES -> E + P}$$

### 3.4 Nucleic Acids (กรดนิวคลีอิก)

#### (a) Nucleotide
Composed of a **nitrogenous base** + a **pentose sugar** + a **phosphate group**.

**Bases:**
- **Purines (วงคู่):** Adenine (A), Guanine (G).
- **Pyrimidines (วงเดี่ยว):** Cytosine (C), Thymine (T, in DNA), Uracil (U, in RNA).

**Sugars:** deoxyribose (DNA), ribose (RNA).

#### (b) DNA
- Two strands forming a **double helix**.
- **Base pairing:** A–T (2 H-bonds), G–C (3 H-bonds).
- Backbone: sugar–phosphate.

#### (c) RNA
- Single-stranded.
- Contains U instead of T.
- Types: mRNA, tRNA, rRNA.

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Classifying a Biomolecule

> Which substance is a carbohydrate: $\ce{C12H22O11}$, $\ce{C3H5(OH)3}$, protein, cholesterol?

**Solution:** $\ce{C12H22O11}$ (a disaccharide — sucrose).

### Type 2: Energy from Fats

> How much energy does 1 mol of triglyceride provide? (Use 1 g fat = 9 kcal.)

**Solution:** $\text{MW triglyceride} \approx 800\ \text{g/mol} \rightarrow 800 \times 9 = 7200\ \text{kcal/mol}$.

### Type 3: Base Pairing in DNA

> One DNA strand contains 30% A. What percentage of T does the complementary strand contain?

**Solution:** A on one strand = T on the other → **30% T**.

### Type 4: Peptide from Amino Acids

> Alanine ($\ce{C3H7NO2}$) + Glycine ($\ce{C2H5NO2}$) → dipeptide + ?

**Solution:**

$$\ce{C3H7NO2 + C2H5NO2 -> C5H10N2O3 + H2O}$$

(loss of 1 H₂O)
