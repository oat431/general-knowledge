# Intermolecular Forces — แรงระหว่างโมเลกุล

> *"The difference between a solid, a liquid, and a gas is not the molecules themselves, but how strongly they hold on to each other."* — Peter Atkins

Intermolecular forces (แรงระหว่างโมเลกุล, IMFs) are the attractive forces between molecules — not within them. These forces influence the state of matter, boiling point, melting point, solubility, and other physical properties of substances.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Intermolecular vs Intramolecular Forces (แรงระหว่างโมเลกุล vs พันธะเคมี)

| Property | Intramolecular (พันธะ) | Intermolecular (IMF) |
|---|---|---|
| Strength (ความแรง) | 100-1000 kJ/mol | 0.1-40 kJ/mol |
| Location (ตำแหน่ง) | Within a molecule (ภายในโมเลกุล) | Between molecules (ระหว่างโมเลกุล) |
| Effect (ผลต่อ) | Molecular structure (โครงสร้างโมเลกุล) | State, BP, MP (สถานะ, จุดเดือด, จุดหลอมเหลว) |

### 3.2 London Dispersion Forces (LDF)

**Mechanism (กลไก):** The motion of electrons creates a temporary induced dipole (induced dipole ชั่วคราว), which in turn induces a dipole in a neighboring molecule.

- Present in all molecules (both polar and nonpolar) (พบในทุกโมเลกุล)
- The only IMF in nonpolar molecules
- **Strength increases with (แรงเพิ่มตาม):** molecular size, molecular mass, number of electrons, polarizability

**Example (ตัวอย่าง):** $\ce{Ne, CH4, CO2, I2}$ → all rely on LDF as the primary IMF.

### 3.3 Dipole-Dipole Forces

Occur between polar molecules — the positive end of one molecule attracts the negative end of another (ขั้วบวกของโมเลกุลหนึ่งดึงดูดขั้วลบของอีกโมเลกุล).

- Stronger than LDF at comparable molecular mass (แรงกว่า LDF เมื่อเปรียบเทียบที่มวลใกล้เคียงกัน)
- Examples: $\ce{HCl, SO2, CHCl3}$

### 3.4 Hydrogen Bonding (H-bond)

Hydrogen bonding is a particularly strong type of dipole-dipole interaction that occurs when:

$$\ce{X-H} \cdots \ce{Y}$$

where $\ce{X, Y}$ = $\ce{N, O, F}$ (high electronegativity, small size) (มี EN สูง, ขนาดเล็ก)

**Examples (ตัวอย่าง):**
- Water $\ce{H2O}$ — H-bond gives an unusually high BP
- $\ce{NH3}$ — BP = -33°C (much higher than $\ce{PH3}$)
- DNA — H-bonds between base pairs (A-T, G-C)

**Effects (ผลกระทบ):**
- Abnormally high boiling/melting points
- Density of ice < density of water (ice floats) (น้ำแข็งลอยน้ำ)
- High surface tension (ความตึงผิวสูง)
- High specific heat capacity (ความจุความร้อนจำเพาะสูง)

### 3.5 Ion-Dipole Forces

Occur between an ion and a polar molecule (เช่น เกลือในน้ำ).

- The strongest of the IMF group (แรงมากที่สุดในกลุ่ม IMF)
- Explains the dissolution of salts in water (อธิบายการละลายของเกลือในน้ำ):

$$\ce{NaCl(s) -> Na^+(aq) + Cl^-(aq)}$$

### 3.6 Summary of IMF Strengths (from weak to strong) (สรุปความแรง IMF จากอ่อนไปแรง)

$$\text{LDF} < \text{Dipole-Dipole} < \text{H-Bond} < \text{Ion-Dipole}$$

(All of these are weaker than ionic and covalent bonds, however.)

### 3.7 Effects on Substance Properties (ผลกระทบต่อสมบัติของสาร)

**Boiling / melting points (จุดเดือด/หลอมเหลว):**
- Stronger IMF → higher BP/MP
- Example: $\ce{H2O}$ (100°C) vs $\ce{H2S}$ (-60°C) — because of H-bonding

**State of matter at room temperature (สถานะของสารที่อุณหภูมิห้อง):**

| IMF | Example BP | State (สถานะ) |
|---|---|---|
| Weak (LDF only) | $\ce{CH4}$ (-161°C) | gas |
| Medium (dipole-dipole) | $\ce{CHCl3}$ (61°C) | liquid |
| Strong (H-bond/ionic) | $\ce{H2O}$ (100°C), $\ce{NaCl}$ (1413°C) | liquid/solid |

**Solubility (การละลาย):** "Like dissolves like"
- Polar solute → polar solvent (e.g., $\ce{NaCl}$ in $\ce{H2O}$)
- Nonpolar solute → nonpolar solvent (e.g., $\ce{I2}$ in $\ce{CCl4}$)
- $\ce{H2O}$ dissolves $\ce{NaCl}$ well because of ion-dipole forces

**Surface tension (ความตึงผิว):** IMF at the surface of a liquid
- $\ce{H2O}$ has high surface tension (H-bonding)
- $\ce{Hg}$ has very high surface tension (metallic bonding)

**Viscosity (ความหนืด):** Increases with IMF strength.

**Vapor pressure (ความดันไอ):** Stronger IMF → harder to evaporate → lower vapor pressure.

### 3.8 Comparing Boiling Points in a Group (เปรียบเทียบ BP ของธาตุหมู่เดียวกัน)

| Period (คาบ) | Group 14 | Group 17 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | $\ce{CH4}$ (-161°C) | $\ce{HF}$ (19.5°C) |
| 3 | $\ce{SiH4}$ (-112°C) | $\ce{HCl}$ (-85°C) |
| 4 | $\ce{GeH4}$ (-88°C) | $\ce{HBr}$ (-67°C) |
| 5 | $\ce{SnH4}$ (-52°C) | $\ce{HI}$ (-35°C) |

**Note (สังเกต):** BP rises as molar mass increases (LDF gets stronger) **except** for $\ce{HF, H2O, NH3}$, whose BPs are anomalously high (because of H-bonding).

### 3.9 Comparing Isomers (การเปรียบเทียบ Isomers)

For the same molecular formula, IMFs are stronger when:
- Surface contact is greater (linear > branched) (พื้นที่ผิวสัมผัสมากขึ้น)
- Dipoles do not cancel (Dipole ไม่หักล้าง)
- H-bonding is possible (H-bond ได้)

Example: $n$-butanol BP = 117°C > isobutanol BP = 108°C (linear has greater surface contact).

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Identifying the Types of IMF

> What IMFs are present in $\ce{CH3OH}$?

**Solution:**

- O–H → **Hydrogen bonding** (primary, หลัก)
- C–O and C–H have EN differences → **Dipole-dipole**
- All molecules have → **London dispersion**

### Type 2: Comparing Boiling Points

> Which has the higher BP: $\ce{CH3OCH3}$ (dimethyl ether) or $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$ (ethanol)?

**Solution:**

- Both have similar molar mass; both have dipole-dipole and LDF.
- $\ce{CH3CH2OH}$ has an **O–H** → H-bonding → **higher BP** (78°C vs -24°C)

### Type 3: Predicting Solubility

> Will $\ce{I2}$ dissolve better in $\ce{CCl4}$ or $\ce{H2O}$?

**Solution:**

- $\ce{I2}$ is **nonpolar** (LDF only)
- "Like dissolves like" → dissolves well in $\ce{CCl4}$ (nonpolar)
- In $\ce{H2O}$ (polar) it dissolves very poorly

### Type 4: Explaining the Anomaly of Water

> Why does $\ce{H2O}$ have a higher BP than $\ce{H2S}$ even though $\ce{H2S}$ has a greater molar mass?

**Solution:**

- $\ce{H2O}$ has **H-bonding** (O is highly electronegative and small) (O มี EN สูง ขนาดเล็ก)
- $\ce{H2S}$ has a large, low-EN S atom → no H-bonding
- H-bonding is much stronger than LDF → $\ce{H2O}$ BP = 100°C, $\ce{H2S}$ BP = -60°C
