# Polymers — พอลิเมอร์

> *"The progress of civilization is measured by the materials at man's disposal — from stone to bronze to iron to polymers."* — Herman Mark

A **polymer (พอลิเมอร์)** is a large molecule (macromolecule) built up from many small units called monomers (มอนอเมอร์) joined by covalent bonds. Polymers are materials of central importance in daily life — from plastics, rubbers, and fibers to proteins and DNA. This note covers classification, synthesis, and recycling.

## 3 | Key Concepts

### 3.1 Monomers vs Polymers (ความแตกต่างระหว่างมอนอเมอร์และพอลิเมอร์)

- **Monomer (มอนอเมอร์):** a small molecule (e.g. ethylene, $\ce{CH2=CH2}$).
- **Polymer (พอลิเมอร์):** a very large molecule built from thousands of monomers.

$$n\ \text{monomer} \longrightarrow (\text{monomer})_n \quad (n = \text{degree of polymerization})$$

### 3.2 Addition Polymerization (Chain-Growth)

Monomers contain a **C=C double bond** → the double bond opens → monomers link into a chain, with no by-product.

#### (a) Polyethylene (PE)

$$n\ce{CH2=CH2 ->[O2 \text{ or } \text{catalyst}] (-CH2-CH2-)_{n}}$$

Used for plastic bags and bottles.

#### (b) Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)

$$n\ce{CH2=CHCl -> (-CH2-CHCl-)_{n}}$$

Used for water pipes and window frames.

#### (c) Polystyrene (PS)

$$n\ce{CH2=CH(C6H5) -> (-CH2-CH(C6H5)-)_{n}}$$

Used for foam and packaging.

#### (d) Polypropylene (PP)

$$n\ce{CH2=CH(CH3) -> (-CH2-CH(CH3)-)_{n}}$$

Used for rope and containers.

#### (e) Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE, Teflon)

$$n\ce{CF2=CF2 -> (-CF2-CF2-)_{n}}$$

Used as a non-stick coating for cookware.

### 3.3 Condensation Polymerization (Step-Growth)

Monomers have ≥ 2 functional groups → they link together **releasing a by-product** (water, HCl, NH₃).

#### (a) Nylon (Polyamide)

Formed from a diamine + a dicarboxylic acid.

$$n\ce{H2N-(CH2)6-NH2 + nHOOC-(CH2)4-COOH -> [-NH-(CH2)6-NH-CO-(CH2)4-CO-]_{n} + (2n-1)H2O}$$

(nylon-6,6)

#### (b) Polyester (Dacron, PET)

Formed from a diol + a dicarboxylic acid.

$$n\ce{HOCH2CH2OH + nHOOC-C6H4-COOH -> [-OCH2CH2-O-CO-C6H4-CO-]_{n} + (2n-1)H2O}$$

#### (c) Phenol-Formaldehyde (Bakelite)

$$\ce{C6H5OH + HCHO ->[-] \text{phenol-formaldehyde resin}}$$

### 3.4 Natural Polymers (พอลิเมอร์ธรรมชาติ)

#### (1) Proteins (โปรตีน)
- Monomer: **α-amino acids** (20 kinds in nature).
- Linkage: **peptide bond** $\ce{-CO-NH-}$.
- Structure: primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary.

#### (2) Carbohydrates (คาร์โบไฮเดรต)
- **Starch (สตาร์ช):** $\ce{(C6H10O5)_n}$, α-1,4-glycosidic linkages; energy storage in plants.
- **Cellulose (เซลลูโลส):** $\ce{(C6H10O5)_n}$, β-1,4-glycosidic linkages; plant cell-wall structure.
- **Glycogen:** energy storage in animals.

#### (3) Nucleic Acids (กรดนิวคลีอิก)
- **DNA:** deoxyribose + phosphate + bases (A,T,G,C); double helix.
- **RNA:** ribose + phosphate + bases (A,U,G,C); single strand.

#### (4) Natural Rubber
- **Polyisoprene (cis-1,4):** $\ce{(-CH2-C(CH3)=CH-CH2-)_{n}}$
- From latex → vulcanization (adding S) → hard rubber.

### 3.5 Properties of Polymers (สมบัติของพอลิเมอร์)

|| Type | Response to heat | Examples |
||---|---|---|
|| **Thermoplastic (เทอร์โมพลาสติก)** | softens and hardens reversibly | PE, PVC, PP, PS |
|| **Thermosetting (เทอร์โมเซตติง)** | permanently hard, does not melt | Bakelite, Melamine |

## 4 | Common Problem Types

### Type 1: Writing a Polymerization Reaction

> Write the polymerization reaction of propene.

**Solution:**

$$n\ce{CH3-CH=CH2 -> (-CH2-CH(CH3)-)_{n}} \text{ (polypropylene)}$$

### Type 2: Classifying a Polymer

> Is PET an addition or condensation polymer?

**Solution:** **Condensation** (releases water when terephthalic acid reacts with ethylene glycol).

### Type 3: Distinguishing Starch, Cellulose, and Protein

> Iodine solution turns a sample blue-black. Which substance is it?

**Solution:** **Starch** (cellulose and protein do not give a color with iodine).

### Type 4: Calculating the Degree of Polymerization

> A polyethylene sample has M = 280,000 g/mol and M(ethylene) = 28 g/mol. Find n.

**Solution:**

$$n = \frac{280{,}000}{28} = 10{,}000$$
